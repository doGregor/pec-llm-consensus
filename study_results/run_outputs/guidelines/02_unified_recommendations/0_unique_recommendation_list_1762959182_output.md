Based on the provided list of recommendations, the following table presents the deduplicated and unified clinical advice for the treatment of penile cancer.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation | Sources |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **Definitive diagnosis must be achieved through tissue sampling/excision.** A pre-treatment biopsy of the primary lesion is required, especially when malignancy is not clinically obvious or when non-surgical treatment (e.g., topical agents, laser, radiotherapy) is planned. | AUA2020; DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **A thorough physical examination of the primary lesion is required** to assess and record its size, location, morphology, and involvement of adjacent structures like the corporal body or urethra. | AUA2020; ESMO2024 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **A physical examination of both groins must be performed to palpate for lymphadenopathy.** The number, laterality, and characteristics (e.g., mobile, fixed) of any palpable or suspicious inguinal nodes should be recorded. | AUA2020; EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **Perform magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or penile ultrasound (US) of the primary tumor** when there is uncertainty regarding corporal invasion (T3) and/or the feasibility of organ-sparing surgery. | AUA2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **Computerized tomography (CT) of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis is performed to evaluate for distant metastatic spread.** For high-risk or node-positive disease, ¹⁸F-FDG-PET/CT can also be used. | AUA2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | Cross-sectional imaging (CT, PET/CT) for primary staging should generally be reserved for T3/T4 tumors or in cases of positive lymph node status. | DGU2020 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | For superficial, non-invasive penile carcinomas (Carcinoma in situ), a fluorescence-diagnostic technique can be used to better determine the extent of the lesion. | DGU2020 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **For clinically node-negative (cN0) low-risk tumors (PeIN, pTa, pT1a G1), surveillance of the inguinal lymph nodes is recommended** instead of operative staging. | DGU2020; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | For clinically node-negative (cN0) intermediate-risk tumors (T1a G2), surveillance may be discussed as an alternative to surgical staging with patients who are willing to comply with strict follow-up. | EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **For clinically node-negative (cN0) intermediate/high-risk tumors (≥pT1b, or high grade), invasive surgical lymph node staging is recommended.** | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **The preferred method for surgical staging in cN0 patients is dynamic sentinel node biopsy (DSNB).** If DSNB is unavailable or not feasible, modified inguinal lymph node dissection (ILND) is an alternative. If DSNB is positive, a completion ILND is recommended. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | If DSNB is planned, perform inguinal ultrasound (US) first, with fine needle aspiration cytology (FNAC) of any sonographically abnormal lymph nodes. | EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | In cN0 patients undergoing staging, frozen section analysis should not be performed on tissue from a modified inguinal lymphadenectomy or DSNB. | DGU2020 |
| **Diagnosis & Staging** | **For clinically node-positive (cN+) disease, an (image-guided) biopsy of the suspicious node is required to confirm metastasis before initiating treatment.** | EAU-ASCO2025; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (General Principles)** | **Patients with early-stage, low-grade, and non-invasive tumors should be offered organ- and function-preserving therapy,** depending on the tumor stage and differentiation grade. | AUA2020; DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (General Principles)** | **The goal of surgical treatment is complete excision with negative margins (R0).** A margin of 5 mm for G1/G2 tumors and 10 mm for G3 tumors is suggested. In case of a positive surgical margin, a re-excision should be performed. | AUA2020; DGU2020 |
| **Primary Treatment (General Principles)** | **Offer patients a balanced and individualized discussion on the benefits and harms of possible treatment options.** Inform them of the higher risk of local recurrence when using organ-sparing treatments compared to amputative surgery. | EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Topical Therapy)** | **Topical therapy with 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) or imiquimod can be offered to patients with biopsy-confirmed superficial disease (PeIN/Tis, Ta).** Patients should be informed about limited efficacy and potential off-label use. | AUA2020; DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Topical Therapy)** | **If topical treatment fails, it should not be repeated.** | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Topical Therapy)** | Example regimens for topical therapy include: <br> • **Imiquimod 5% cream:** Apply at night three times per week for 4–16 weeks. <br> • **5-FU cream 5%:** Apply twice daily for 2–6 weeks. | NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Topical Therapy)** | Topical therapy is ineffective for more advanced stages of penile cancer. | AUA2020 |
| **Primary Treatment (Laser Therapy)** | **Laser ablation (e.g., CO₂ or Nd:YAG laser) can be used for biopsy-confirmed superficial or early-stage tumors (PeIN/Tis, Ta, T1).** | AUA2020; DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Laser Therapy)** | A surgical margin of 5 mm is considered oncologically safe for laser therapy. | AUA2020 |
| **Primary Treatment (Post-Therapy Biopsy)** | **After topical or laser therapy, a control biopsy should be performed to verify local tumor control,** especially in cases of clinical doubt. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | **Organ-sparing surgery options (circumcision, wide local excision, glansectomy, Mohs) are offered for lesions confined to the glans and prepuce (PeIN, Ta, T1-T2)** in patients willing to comply with strict follow-up. | EAU-ASCO2025; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | **Intra-operative frozen section analysis of resection margins should be performed** to confirm negative margins, especially in cases of doubt. | EAU-ASCO2025; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | **Circumcision can be a curative treatment for tumors confined to the prepuce (PeIN/Tis, Ta).** | AUA2020; DGU2020; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | When using radiotherapy or topical therapy for a preputial lesion, a circumcision should be performed first. | DGU2020; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | **Wide local excision (WLE) is an option for early-stage tumors (PeIN, Ta, T1).** Complete excision with wide negative margins is required. | DGU2020; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | **Mohs micrographic surgery can be considered as an alternative to WLE for select, low-grade, superficial lesions,** particularly on the glans or distal penis, if the appropriate expertise is available. | AUA2020; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | **Glansectomy (partial or total) or glans resurfacing are options for tumors located on the glans penis (PeIN, Ta, T1-T2).** Reconstruction may be performed with a split-thickness skin graft. | AUA2020; DGU2020; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Surgical)** | **Partial or total penectomy is recommended for patients with higher stage tumors (T2, T3, T4), particularly with invasion of the corpora cavernosa,** unfavorable histopathology, or for those not suitable for organ-sparing approaches. | AUA2020; DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Radiotherapy)** | **Radiotherapy (often brachytherapy) can be an alternative to surgery for selected patients with T1-T2 tumors (<4 cm),** particularly those who are unfit for surgery. Patients should be informed of a potentially lower local control rate compared to surgery. | AUA2020; DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Radiotherapy)** | For T3–4 or N+ surgically unresectable disease, consider EBRT with concurrent chemotherapy to the penis and regional lymph nodes. | NCCN2025 |
| **Primary Treatment (Radiotherapy)** | Specific radiotherapy doses may include: <br> • **EBRT:** 66-74 Gy in 2 Gy fractions. <br> • **Brachytherapy (HDR):** 42-45 Gy in 12-14 fractions. <br> • **Brachytherapy (LDR/PDR):** 60-65 Gy delivered continuously over ~5 days. | ESMO2024 |
| **Primary Treatment (Multimodal)** | For non-resectable, locally advanced primary tumors (T4), consider induction/neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by surgery for responders, or chemo-radiotherapy. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Recurrent Disease** | **Local recurrence after organ-sparing surgery should be treated according to the principles of primary tumor management.** Small, non-invasive recurrences can be treated with salvage organ-sparing options. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Recurrent Disease** | For large local recurrences or those with invasion of the corpora cavernosa, partial or total penectomy is warranted. | AUA2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
| **Recurrent Disease** | After radiotherapy, local recurrence should be treated with surgical salvage therapy. | DGU2020 |
| **Recurrent Disease** | After topical or laser therapy, local recurrence should be treated with a different treatment modality. | DGU2020 |
| **Recurrent Disease** | Regional lymph node recurrence after initial surveillance should be managed according to the same principles as primary lymph node metastases (i.e., with ILND +/- systemic therapy). | DGU2020; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Recurrent Disease** | Regional lymph node recurrence after a prior radical lymphadenectomy should be managed with a multimodal approach, including neoadjuvant chemotherapy, surgery, and/or radiotherapy. | DGU2020; ESMO2024 |
| **Lymph Node Management** | Following circumcision for a primary tumor, patients with moderately or poorly differentiated tumors (grades 2 and 3) will require inguinal node dissection. | AUA2020 |
| **Lymph Node Management** | For palpable, mobile inguinal nodes (cN1-cN2), inguinal lymph node dissection (ILND) is recommended. The dissection may be fascial-sparing or radical, with preservation of the saphenous vein where possible. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Lymph Node Management** | If intraoperatively suspicious nodes are removed during surgery for cN+ disease, frozen section analysis should be performed. If positive, an ipsilateral radical lymphadenectomy should be completed in the same session. | DGU2020 |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **For bulky, fixed, or extensive inguinal nodes (cN3), neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) with a cisplatin- and taxane-based regimen is preferred over up-front surgery.** | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Lymph Node Management** | Minimally-invasive ILND for cN1–2 disease should only be offered as part of a clinical trial. It is contraindicated for cN3 disease. | EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Following a response to neoadjuvant chemotherapy, patients should undergo consolidative surgery,** including radical inguinal lymphadenectomy and possible pelvic lymphadenectomy. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Ipsilateral pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND) is recommended for patients with ≥2-3 positive inguinal nodes on one side or with extranodal extension.** | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | **Recommended neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens are cisplatin- and taxane-based triplets,** such as TIP (Paclitaxel/Ifosfamide/Cisplatin) or TPF (Cisplatin/Paclitaxel/5-Fluorouracil). | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | **Adjuvant chemotherapy should be considered for patients with pN2 or pN3 disease** following lymph node dissection. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | Adjuvant radiotherapy (with or without chemo-sensitization) should be offered or considered for patients with high-risk pathological findings such as pN2/N3 disease or extranodal extension. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | Definitive radiotherapy (with or without chemo sensitization) can be offered to patients who are unwilling or unable to undergo surgery. | EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | **For first-line treatment of metastatic disease, platinum-based combination chemotherapy is the preferred approach.** Recommended regimens include TIP, TPF, or cisplatin + 5-FU. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | **Bleomycin-containing chemotherapy regimens should not be used** due to the risk of pulmonary toxicity. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; NCCN2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | For patients with progressive disease, enrollment in a clinical trial is the preferred option for subsequent-line therapy. | EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Systemic Therapy** | Subsequent-line therapy options may include paclitaxel monotherapy or, for biomarker-selected tumors (MSI-H, dMMR, TMB-H), immunotherapy such as pembrolizumab. | DGU2020; ESMO2024; NCCN2025 |
| **Palliative & Supportive Care** | Patients with metastatic disease or requiring multimodal therapy should be discussed in an interdisciplinary tumor board. | DGU2020 |
| **Palliative & Supportive Care** | Palliative radiotherapy can be offered for symptom control in advanced disease (e.g., 30 Gy in 10 fractions). | EAU-ASCO2025; NCCN2025 |
| **Palliative & Supportive Care** | For chronic lymphedema resulting from treatment, a complex decongestive therapy (lymphatic drainage, compression therapy, and skin care) should be performed. | DGU2020 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | **Close, regular, and risk-stratified follow-up is crucial after treatment,** especially for the first 2-5 years, to detect recurrences and functional disorders. | AUA2020; DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | A general follow-up schedule is every 3-4 months for the first 2 years, then less frequently. Follow-up should extend for at least 5 years. | AUA2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | Specific follow-up schedules after laser therapy are: <br> • **CO2 Laser:** After 2 months, then every 3 months for 3 years, then biannually. <br> • **Nd:YAG Laser:** Every 3 months for 2 years, then biannually. | AUA2020 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | Specific follow-up schedules based on treatment: <br>• **Organ Preservation:** Every 3 mo (Yrs 1-2), every 6 mo (Yrs 3-5). <br>• **Total Penectomy:** Every 3 mo (Yrs 1-2), annually (Yrs 3-5). | DGU2020 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | **Patients should be taught self-examination** for early detection of recurrence and given easy access back to the clinic if concerns arise. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | Penile cancer care should be delivered by an extended multi-disciplinary team including specialists in urology, oncology, nursing, pathology, radiology, psychology, and lymphedema therapy. | EAU-ASCO2025 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | **Patients should be offered psychological support and counseling,** and physicians should discuss the psychological and functional (urinary, sexual) impact of the disease and its treatments. | EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
| **Follow-up & Survivorship** | Patients should be assessed for lymphedema at follow-up and referred to lymphedema therapists early if it develops. | DGU2020; EAU-ASCO2025; ESMO2024 |
