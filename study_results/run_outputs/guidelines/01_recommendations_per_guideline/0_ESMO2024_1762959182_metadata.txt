**Extraction of Penile Cancer Treatment Recommendations**

Alright, let's break down this guideline. My task is to distill explicit treatment recommendations into a usable format. I've gone through the document meticulously, focusing on sections dedicated to recommendations and management of specific disease stages. Here's a summary of the key findings, organized by the aspect of care:

**Diagnosis & Staging**

*   Clinical assessment of the primary tumour and inguinal nodes, along with imaging studies like MRI and US, are fundamental.
*   CT is advised for all cases to rule out distant metastases.
*   FNAC should be used for clinically impalpable inguinal nodes. Palpable inguinal nodes necessitate percutaneous FNAC.

**Primary Tumour Management**

*   **PeIN:** Circumcision is mandatory initially. Residual PeIN can be treated topically (5-FU or imiquimod) or with CO2 laser ablation. Wide local excision or glans resurfacing is considered if topical treatment fails.
*   **Ta-1:** Circumcision for tumours on the foreskin. Wide local excision or advancement flap for small glans tumours is preferred. Glans resurfacing is recommended for PeIN or T1a lesions. Mohs micrographic surgery may be used for small, low-grade penile lesions. Brachytherapy is an option for pT1 lesions.
*   **T2:** Glansectomy (with or without distal urethrectomy) is the surgical treatment of choice for T2 tumours on the glans penis. Partial or total penectomy is a valid alternative if adequate surgical margins cannot be ensured. Brachytherapy is also an option for low-volume T1-T2 tumours.
*   **T3:** Partial or total penectomy combined with a perineal urethrostomy are the treatments of choice.
*   **T4:** Total penectomy with perineal urethrostomy is the recommended option.

**Regional Lymph Node Management**

*   **cN0 (Low-risk):** Managed with clinical surveillance.
*   **cN0 (Intermediate/High-risk):** Active treatment is recommended. DSLNB is the preferred approach. If DSLNB is unavailable, modified ILND can be performed.
*   **cN1-2:** Radical inguinal lymphadenectomy is indicated if metastatic disease is confirmed.
*   **cN3 (Bulky/Ulcerated Disease):** Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (ChT) followed by ipsilateral radical inguinal and pelvic lymphadenectomy in responders is the recommended strategy.
*   **Pelvic LN Dissection:** Unilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy is indicated for patients with two or more ipsilateral inguinal metastases, metastasis diameter ≥30 mm, or extranodal extension.
*   **Salvage ILND:** For recurrent inguinal disease without distant metastases, salvage inguinal lymphadenectomy with myocutaneous flap reconstruction is an option. Multimodal treatment with neoadjuvant or adjuvant ChT is advised in this situation.

**Systemic Therapy (Locally Advanced & Metastatic)**

*   **Neoadjuvant ChT:**  Considered for T4, inoperable primary, or fixed cN3 disease. A triplet regimen such as TIP or TPF should be discussed.
*   **Adjuvant ChT:** Recommended for high-risk patients (pN2-pN3) after lymph node dissection. Four cycles of TIP or TPF should be considered.
*   **Post-operative RT:** Recommended for pN3 disease (50 Gy in 2 Gy fractions), potentially with higher doses (up to 66 Gy) if clinically indicated. Adjuvant chemotherapy is also advised.
*   **Responders to Neoadjuvant ChT:** Should undergo consolidation surgery.
*   **Non-responders to Neoadjuvant ChT:** May consider further ChT, RT, or a clinical trial.

**Recurrent Disease Management**

*   **Recurrent (Local):** Penile-sparing surgery for non-invasive recurrence. Partial or total penectomy for invasive recurrence.
*   **Recurrent (Regional):** Systemic ChT, external beam radiotherapy (EBRT), surgery, or a combination of these modalities.
*   **Metastatic:** Systemic ChT is the primary approach (platinum-based combinations like cisplatin-5FU, carboplatin-paclitaxel, TIP, or TPF). Participation in a clinical trial is strongly recommended.
*   **Metastatic (Second-line):** For non-responders to first-line ChT, consider second-line ChT, RT for local control, best supportive care, or enrollment in a clinical trial.
*   **Palliative RT:** Used for sites requiring local control.

**Follow-up Care**

*   Close follow-up every 3-4 months for the initial two years following primary surgery is crucial to detect local recurrence.
