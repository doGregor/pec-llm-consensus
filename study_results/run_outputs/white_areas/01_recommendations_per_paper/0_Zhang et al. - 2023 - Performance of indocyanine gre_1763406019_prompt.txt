# **Performance of indocyanine green in sentinel lymph node mapping ** **and lymph node metastasis in penile cancer: systematic review, ** **meta‑analysis, and single‑center experience**

**Rui Zhang** **[1]** **· Yaqin Zhang** **[2]** **· Sihan Dong** **[1]** **· Kai Pang** **[3]** **· Xiaofeng Yang** **[4]** **· Xunbin Wei** **[1,5]**

Received: 10 March 2023 / Accepted: 7 June 2023 / Published online: 7 July 2023
© The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany, part of Springer Nature 2023

**Abstract**
**Purpose** The aim of this study was to investigate the overall sensitivity and specificity of indocyanine green (ICG)-nearinfrared (NIR) fluorescence imaging in the detection of sentinel lymph node metastasis (SLNM) in penile cancer.
**Methods**
We searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library databases to identify manuscripts where ICG was intravenously administered prior to or during penile cancer surgery, with no restriction on language
or publication status. The results extracted are presented as forest plots.
**Results**
Seven studies were included in the analysis. The median sensitivity and specificity of ICG-NIR imaging for SLNM
detection were 100 and 4%, respectively; the pooled sensitivity was 100.0% (95% confidence interval [CI] 97.0–100.0) and
specificity was 2.0% (95% CI 1.0–3.0). There was no significant difference in the diagnostic results between different injection sites and doses in each experimental group.
**Conclusion**
To our knowledge, this meta-analysis is the first to summarize the diagnostic performance of ICG-NIR imaging
for SLNM detection in penile cancer. ICG is sensitive in the imaging of SLN tissue, which can consequently improve the
accuracy of lymph node detection. However, the specificity is very low.

**Methods**

**Protocol and registration**

Before performing this analysis, the protocol was registered in the international prospective register of systematic
reviews (PROSPERO), with the number CRD42022375235.

**Search strategy**

A systematic search was performed of the PubMed, Embase,
Web of Science, Scopus, and the Cochrane Library databases, with no language or publication status restriction.
The search strategy included the following keywords:
‘‘Penile Neoplasms OR Penile Cancer OR Penile carcinoma
OR penile malignancy OR Penis cancer OR penis malignancy OR penis carcinoma’’ and ‘‘lymph node OR LN OR
lymphatic OR sentinel node’’ and “indocyanine green OR
ICG.” The following medical subject headings (MeSH)
were also used: indocyanine green, lymph nodes, and penile
neoplasms. Duplicate studies were first removed, and the
included studies were systematically screened based on the
title and abstract, followed by a full-text screening.
Additionally, we searched the relevant studies included in
the references. The last search was performed on November
13, 2022.

**Study selection**

The main index was the detection of positive SLNs in penile
cancer using ICG-NIR fluorescence imaging.
The inclusion criteria involved studies that contained the

following: (1) patients who had resectable penile neoplasms;
(2) patients who underwent surgery, with ICG being used to
locate the SLN; (3) patients who underwent postoperative
pathological examination; and (4) sample sizes of more than
five patients.
The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) case reports,
meta-analyses, animal studies, abstracts, and reviews; (2)

unavailable data and lack of sufficient detail disclosure

regarding the pathological outcome of lymph nodes.

**Data extraction and quality assessment**

Two authors independently screened the articles according
to the inclusion and exclusion criteria; any disagreements
between the two authors were resolved by a third author.

**Discussion**

In 1958, ICG was approved by the US Food and Drug
Administration for clinical application, including in liver
function measurement, retinal angiography, and cardiac
function measurement. In recent years, ICG has been used
in coronary artery bypass surgery, intestinal anastomosis,
and skin flap reconstruction [18–20]. When ICG is injected
intravenously, it first binds to plasma proteins, allowing them
to remain in the blood vessels. In plasma, the wavelength
of its emission spectrum is 830 nm, which avoids the influence of its own fluorescence and can penetrate 10–15 mm of
tissue. ICG is metabolized by the liver into bile and almost
completely metabolized after 8 min. When injected intravenously, ICG binds to protein and usually reaches the nearest
draining lymph node within 15 min [21, 22]. The ability of
ICG-NIR fluorescence to detect metastatic lymph nodes in
various types of malignancies has been extensively studied
in several trials. However, it is important to note that the
sensitivity of ICG-NIR fluorescence varies depending on the
site of the primary malignant tumor. The sensitivity of ICG
fluorescence is 50–100% for endometrial cancer and cervical cancer, 50–100% for gastric cancer, 75% for esophageal
cancer, and 95–100% for breast cancer; the overall detection
rate thereof is 88.6–100% [23–25]. Current evidence on the
role of ICG in SLN imaging of penile cancer is limited to
case series or relatively small, single-center cohort studies.
This review included seven studies. Despite including all
existing studies in this field, the total number of patients in
these seven studies was only 638. According to the results of
the analysis, we found that the application of ICG can help
physicians identify the SLN tissue of penile cancer intraoperatively, with sensitivity up to 100%; however, it has low
specificity for detecting tumor metastasis in SLN, with a
maximum of 40% and a total of only 2%. Combining the

The authors of the included studies used different injection doses, injection sites, imaging equipment, and imaging

except for specificity, which may be owing to the following
reasons. First, the concentration of ICG required for NIR

result in systemic distribution of ICG. Therefore, there was
no significant difference in the results with local injection.
However, in the study by Hora et al., the total amount of
ICG injected in one patient was 1.25 mg, so the lymph nodes
were not visible. On the contrary, in the study by Torbrand

head of the penis to the root and drained to both sides at the
pubic symphysis site; therefore, no significant difference was
found in the localization of bilateral inguinal lymph nodes

location of the bilateral inguinal lymph nodes was relatively
shallow, which did not require a high sensitivity and detection depth of imaging equipment. Moreover, the addition


of pseudo-color to NIR imaging equipment could greatly
improve detection with the human eye. Fourth, ICG was
introduced into the nearest lymph node within about 15 min
and remained in the lymph tissue for a long time, making it
detectable within a long imaging window.
Because ICG is used for lymph node localization and
imaging through lymph node drainage, different tumor
stages may lead to different degrees of lymphatic vessel
obstruction, leading to different detection results. However,
this aspect was not researched in the existing studies or this
paper. Thus, further studies are needed to investigate the
efficacy of ICG in patients with different tumor stages.

**Conclusion**

Here, we investigated the use of ICG for SLN localization and imaging in penile cancer. The main finding was
that ICG had a good recognition effect of SLNs in penile
cancer, which could provide location information of lymph
nodes to guide physicians in better performing lymph
node excision. However, our findings also showed that
the specificity and accuracy of judging whether there was
tumor metastasis were very low. Another limitation of this
study was that accuracy of the data was influenced by the
injected dose. As a result, high-quality studies involving
more patients are necessary before ICG imaging can be
recommended as a standard test in daily clinical practice.
## 1 3


Further research is also needed to explore how different
tumor stages affect the efficacy of ICG imaging and to
compare it with other methods such as blue dye and radioactive colloid. This paper contributed to the existing literature by providing a systematic review and meta-analysis of
ICG imaging in penile cancer and highlighting its potential
benefits and challenges.
