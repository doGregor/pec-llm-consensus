| **Lymph Node Management** <br> (Surgical Approach) | **Recommendation:** Minimally Invasive Inguinal Lymphadenectomy (MIS-ILND), including video-endoscopic (VEIL) and robot-assisted (RA-VEIL) techniques, is recommended over the traditional Open Inguinal Lymphadenectomy (O-ILND) for managing inguinal nodal disease in penile cancer.<br><br>**Specific Clinical Indications:**<br>The recommendation is applicable to a broad range of patients indicated for inguinal lymph node dissection, including:<br>• **Prophylactic intent:** In patients with high-risk primary tumors and non-palpable nodes (e.g., cN0).<br>• **Therapeutic intent:** In patients with clinically palpable and enlarged inguinal nodes confirmed to be metastatic (e.g., cN1, cN2, cN1-3).<br><br>**Precise Conditions:**<br>• This approach is particularly supported for broader adoption in **high-volume centers with the requisite surgical expertise** to ensure optimal outcomes.<br><br>**Additional Clinical Clarifications:**<br>• MIS-ILND is associated with significantly fewer minor (Clavien-Dindo 1-2) and major (Clavien-Dindo >2) complications, particularly lower wound infection rates, and results in shorter hospitalization.<br>• This approach achieves comparable oncological efficacy to O-ILND, with no significant differences in lymph node yield, local groin recurrence rates, or overall survival. |
| **Lymph Node Management** <br> (Advanced Disease) | **Recommendation:** In patients with advanced disease requiring both inguinal and ipsilateral pelvic lymph node dissection, MIS-ILND is an advantageous approach.<br><br>**Specific Clinical Indications:**<br>• Patients with penile cancer who require concomitant ipsilateral pelvic lymph node dissection in addition to inguinal lymphadenectomy.<br><br>**Additional Clinical Clarifications:**<br>• The MIS-ILND approach can avoid the need for a separate, additional abdominal incision that is often required when performing an open pelvic lymph node dissection. |
