Based on the systematic review provided, the following treatment recommendations for Adrenocortical Carcinoma (ACC) are identified:

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General Management** | Patients should be referred to high-volume centers and managed with a multi-disciplinary approach. |
| **Primary Treatment (Localized Disease)** | The standard of care for suspected localized ACC is upfront open adrenalectomy and loco-regional lymphadenectomy with negative margins. |
| **Primary Treatment (Localized Disease)** | To achieve R0 resection, tumor capsule rupture should be avoided, and en bloc resection of macroscopically invaded adjacent organs should be considered. |
| **Primary Treatment (Localized Disease)** | Laparoscopic adrenalectomy (LA) is considered an alternative approach only for suspected ACC with a diameter of less than 6cm, without lymph node involvement or local invasion. |
| **Neoadjuvant Therapy** | Neoadjuvant therapy is not recommended by guidelines unless in the context of clinical trials. |
| **Neoadjuvant Therapy** | For locally advanced disease, neoadjuvant treatment can be considered if cytoreduction is needed for surgery, following discussion in a multidisciplinary tumor board. |
| **Adjuvant Therapy** | Adjuvant mitotane monotherapy is recommended only for high-risk patients after surgery (defined as stage III disease, R1 resection, or Ki-67 > 10%). |
| **Adjuvant Therapy** | For patients receiving mitotane, blood monitoring is recommended, and the treatment duration should be at least 2 years but not more than 5 years. |
| **Adjuvant Therapy** | Adjuvant mitotane should not be used routinely for low to intermediate-risk ACC patients (stage I-III, R0 surgery, Ki-67 ≤ 10%). |
| **Adjuvant Therapy** | Adjuvant radiotherapy should be considered on an individualized basis for patients with R1 resection or stage III disease, offered in addition to mitotane. |
| **Adjuvant Therapy** | Adjuvant cytotoxic chemotherapy is not recommended, as there is no evidence to support its use. |
| **Systemic Therapy (First-Line Advanced/Metastatic)** | The combination of cisplatin, etoposide, doxorubicin, and mitotane is the preferred option for first-line treatment. |
| **Systemic Therapy (Advanced/Metastatic)** | Mitotane monotherapy is recommended for patients with a low volume of disease, a limited number of metastatic sites, or those with later recurrence after surgery. |
| **Systemic Therapy (Advanced/Metastatic)** | Mitotane should be combined with cytotoxic cisplatin-based chemotherapy in patients with extensive and rapidly progressive disease. |
| **Systemic Therapy (Advanced/Metastatic)** | Pembrolizumab is indicated as a treatment option (based on consensus and NCCN guidelines). |
| **Surgical & Local Management (Advanced/Metastatic)** | Debulking surgery and metastasectomy can be considered for selected patients, especially those with >90% removable disease and functional tumors. |
| **Surgical & Local Management (Advanced/Metastatic)** | Consolidative surgery with resection of residual disease should be considered in selected patients who achieve a deep and sustained response after systemic therapy. |
| **Surgical & Local Management (Advanced/Metastatic)** | Ablative treatments (SBRT, thermal therapies, liver-directed therapy) should be indicated on a case-by-case basis as part of an individualized strategy. |
| **Genetic/Molecular Testing** | Genetic counseling and testing for inherited genetic syndromes (germline) are recommended. |
| **Genetic/Molecular Testing** | Tumor testing for MSI, MMR, and TMB using an FDA-approved test should be considered. |
| **Genetic/Molecular Testing** | Next-Generation Sequencing (NGS) testing should be performed when available for the use of agnostic-approved drugs. |
