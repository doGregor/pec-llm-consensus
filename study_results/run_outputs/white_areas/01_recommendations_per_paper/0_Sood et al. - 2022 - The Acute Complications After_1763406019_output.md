Based on the provided systematic review, the following explicit treatment and management recommendations for penile cancer are extracted and presented in the required format.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Bilateral Inguinal Lymph Node Dissection (ILND) - Standard Indication:**<br>• Considered a standard-of-care option for:<br>  &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;1. Patients with clinically node-negative disease (cN0) but with high-risk primary tumors (≥pT1b).<br>  &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;2. Patients with palpable or visible nodal disease on clinical examination or cross-sectional imaging (pTany, N1-3). |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Modified Inguinal Lymph Node Dissection (ILND) - Morbidity Reduction:**<br>• **Indication:** Proposed for patients with high-risk primary penile cancer and clinically negative groins (cN0) to minimize morbidity associated with radical ILND.<br>• **Technique:** Involves limiting the incision size, excluding nodal dissection lateral to the femoral artery (medial dissection), preserving the greater saphenous vein, and eliminating the Sartorius muscle flap.<br>• **Additional Clarification:** Due to recurrence risks, expansion of the modified template (superficial ILND or fascial-sparing dissection) to include more nodal tissue with intraoperative frozen section is suggested. |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Minimally Invasive ILND - Morbidity Reduction:**<br>• **Modality:** Laparoscopic and robotic approaches are proposed as alternatives to open ILND to further minimize morbidity.<br>• **Indication:** Primarily for patients with clinically node-negative disease (cN0). |
| **Intraoperative Care** | **Principles to Reduce Postoperative Complications:**<br>• **Tissue Handling:** Use atraumatic handling of anatomic structures to minimize tissue devitalization.<br>• **Hemostasis/Lymphostasis:** Perform careful control of lymphatics and small vessels (using clips or ties), especially during inguinal surgery.<br>• **Incisions:** Use incisions that follow natural skin creases (Langerhans lines) and avoid S- and T-shaped incisions for inguinal access.<br>• **Flap Creation (ILND):** Create the skin flap underneath the Scarpa fascia to preserve dermal microvasculature.<br>• **Wound Closure (ILND):** Cover the inguinal node dissection site with myocutaneous flaps if primary closure is associated with excess tension or if the patient has received prior radiotherapy. |
| **Perioperative Care** | **Antibiotic Prophylaxis:**<br>• Administer the first dose of prophylactic antibiotics within 1 hour of the surgical incision.<br>• Continue antibiotics for 24 hours after primary penile surgery.<br>• Continue antibiotics until the removal of surgical drains for ILND. |
| **Perioperative Care** | **Thromboprophylaxis (DVT/PE Prevention) for ILND:**<br>• Use perioperative sequential compression devices.<br>• Administer prophylactic low-molecular-weight heparin or direct oral anticoagulants.<br>• Encourage early ambulation. |
| **Perioperative Care** | **General Patient Management:**<br>• Perform early ambulation post-surgery.<br>• Optimize nutritional support, especially for patients with low albumin or poor health status. |
| **Postoperative Care** | **Primary Penile Surgery - Wound and Catheter Management:**<br>• **Dressing:** Use compressive surgical dressings, left in place for 2-7 days to reduce swelling and hematoma.<br>• **Foley Catheter:** Typically remove the Foley catheter around 7 days post-surgery for patients who undergo neomeatus creation or STSG application. |
| **Postoperative Care** | **Inguinal Lymph Node Dissection (ILND) - Specific Management:**<br>• **Lymphedema Prevention:** Use compression stockings.<br>• **Fluid Management:** Conduct daily measurement of "ins/outs" and perform regular stripping of surgical drains.<br>• **Drain Management:** Maintain closed suction drains until output is <30-50 mL per 24 hours. Use a chlorhexidine-impregnated disc at the drain exit site to reduce infection risk.<br>• **Foley Catheter:** Typically remove the Foley catheter on postoperative day 2, unless the patient has a history of significant LUTS or retention. |
| **Complication Management** | **Primary Penile Surgery Complications:**<br>• **Surgical Site Infections (SSIs):** Manage with empirical antibiotics, transitioning to culture-specific therapy, along with regular (e.g., every 12-hour) wet-to-dry dressing changes.<br>• **Meatal Stenosis:** Manage with self-catheterization or formal surgical revision of the neomeatus.<br>• **Partial Skin Graft Loss:** Manage expectantly with observation.<br>• **Total Skin Graft Loss:** Manage with wet-to-dry dressing changes followed by delayed regrafting. |
| **Complication Management** | **Inguinal Surgery Complications - Wound Issues:**<br>• **Infected Inguinal Wounds:** Manage with meticulous wound care, daily inspection, and culture-specific antibiotics.<br>• **Wound Dehiscence / Flap Necrosis:** Manage with wet-to-dry dressings or vacuum-assisted closure (VAC) dressings, followed by delayed plastic surgery closure or healing by secondary intention.<br>• **Patients with Large Body Habitus:** Place dry gauze ("Interdry") in the groin folds to prevent moisture buildup and fungal colonization. |
| **Complication Management** | **Inguinal Surgery Complications - Fluid Collections:**<br>• **Refractory Seroma/Lymphocele:** For excessive fluid collection after drain removal, management may require image-guided drain placement, lymphatic embolization, or operative exploration.<br>• **Lower Extremity Lymphedema:** Minimize risk with saphenous-vein sparing techniques, prevention of SSIs, early use of fitted compression devices, and a preemptive referral to a lymphedema specialist. |
