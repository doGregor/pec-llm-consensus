## The Role of Plastic Surgery in Urologic Cancer and Trauma Reconstruction: A Systematic Review

Alexander F. Dagi, MPhil, Nadeem E. Jones, BA, and Jarrod T. Bogue, MD


Background: The role of plastic surgeons in urologic reconstruction following
malignancy and trauma remains poorly defined despite potential synergies in
complex cases requiring specialized reconstructive techniques.
Methods: Systematic literature search of PubMed, Medline/Ovid, and Cochrane
databases using predetermined search terms combining “plastic surgery” with
urologic cancer and trauma keywords. Studies were included if both urologic
and plastic surgeons were involved in clinical care.
Results: Four studies met inclusion criteria from 46 initial references, representing diverse applications from 2021 to 2025. Key findings included the following:
(1) complex abdominal wall reconstruction following retroperitoneal sarcoma resection using biologic mesh and omental flaps; (2) plastic surgery involvement in
33% of scrotal squamous cell carcinoma and 67% of extramammary Paget's disease cases undergoing Mohs surgery with low recurrence rates; (3) microsurgical
lymphovenous bypass for lymphedema following inguinal lymphadenectomy in
penile cancer patients; and (4) gracilis muscle flap interposition for rectourethral
fistula repair, demonstrating significantly improved healing rates with systematic
multidisciplinary protocols (100% vs 66%, P = 0.04).
Conclusions: Limited but compelling evidence supports plastic surgery involvement in complex urologic reconstruction, particularly for extensive oncologic resections, scrotal malignancies, lymphedema management, and complex fistula repair.
Systematic multidisciplinary approaches demonstrate improved outcomes, although
further research is needed to establish optimal protocols and cost-effectiveness.

Key Words: urologic reconstruction, lymphedema, gracilis muscle flap, uroplastic surgery

RESULTS

The 4 included studies represent diverse applications of plastic surgical expertise in urologic oncology, with publication dates ranging from
2021 to 2025 and all originating from institutions in the United States.
Walker et al described a case of radiation-associated retroperitoneal sarcoma with kidney involvement requiring complex reconstruction
of the abdominal wall using biologic mesh and an omental flap following
multivisceral resection. [2] This case demonstrated the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration involving surgical oncology, orthopedic oncology, thoracic surgery, and plastic surgery.
Roberson et al conducted a retrospective review of 26 patients
undergoing Mohs micrographic surgery for scrotal cutaneous malignancies (16 squamous cell carcinoma lesions, 12 extramammary Paget's disease lesions). [3] Plastic surgery expertise was required for complex reconstruction in approximately one-third of squamous cell carcinoma cases
and two-thirds of Paget's disease cases, with the remainder amenable
to primary closure, local flaps, or healing by secondary intention. Notably, despite the extensive nature of these resections, oncologic recurrence
rates remained low (6% for SCC, 0% for squamous cell carcinoma and
extramammary Paget's disease after achieving negative margins).
Goldman et al presented a case series of 3 patientswith advanced penile squamous cell carcinoma who underwent microsurgical lymphovenous
bypass procedures performed by plastic surgeons to address lower extremity lymphedema following inguinal lymphadenectomy. [4] The authors
demonstrated clinical improvement in lymphedema and quality of life,
highlighting the value of prophylactic or therapeutic microsurgical intervention for lymphatic disruption.
Finally, Brush et al reported outcomes of gracilis muscle flap interposition for complex rectourethral fistula repair in 23 patients between 2001 and 2022. [5] This retrospective quasi-experimental study
compared outcomes before and after implementation of a multidisciplinary treatment algorithm involving plastic surgery consultation.
The algorithm indicated plastic surgery involvement for (1) radiated
rectourethral fistulae less than 3 cm and (2) nonradiated rectourethral
fistulae greater than 2 cm. The frequency of definitive rectourethral fistula healing improved significantly in the postalgorithm group (100%
vs 66%, P = 0.04), demonstrating the value of systematic multidisciplinary collaboration in complex urologic reconstruction.
No studies addressing plastic surgery involvement in urologic
trauma met inclusion criteria.

DISCUSSION

In this systematic review, we sought to assess the literature on
plastic surgeon involvement in urologic reconstruction following cancer
or trauma, specifically to focus attention on interdisciplinary collaboration outside of gender affirming procedures. Our findings highlight the
potential breadth of collaboration between the 2 fields while also
highlighting the paucity of published literature in this domain. The contrast suggests either underutilization of plastic surgical services or
underreporting of such collaborations.

The common theme across the 4 included studies is the added
value of specialized reconstructive techniques in managing complex defects or complications following urologic cancer treatment. Notably,
plastic surgical involvement appears particularly valuable in the following: (1) reconstruction following extensive oncologic resection, (2)
management of scrotal cutaneous malignancies to optimize both oncologic and functional outcomes, (3) addressing lymphedema as a complication of inguinal lymphadenectomy, and (4) complex rectourethral fistula repair using vascularized tissue interposition.
The study by Brush et al provides particularly compelling evidence
for the benefits of systematic multidisciplinary collaboration, demonstrating a statistically significant improvement in healing rates when plastic
surgery involvement was incorporated into a standardized treatment algorithm for complex rectourethral fistulae. [5] This represents the first study to
quantitatively demonstrate improved outcomes through algorithmic implementation of plastic surgery involvement in urologic reconstruction.
Our review did not capture literature on oncologic complications
in kidney transplant recipients, although this represents another domain
where plastic surgery expertise is important. Papadopoulos et al and Ge
et al reported safe and effective management of posttransplant cutaneous malignancies and wound complications using flaps and grafts,
underscoring the role of plastic surgery in complex oncologic and immunosuppressed patient care. [6,7]

Although our review did not identify studies involving urologic
trauma, penile replantation and transplantation exemplify complex reconstructive scenarios where plastic surgical collaboration is critical.
Penile replantation, historically performed without vascular repair and
associated with high rates of necrosis and strictures, now benefits from
microsurgical techniques that improve tissue survival and functional
recovery. [8] Penile transplantation using vascularized composite allografts, performed in select cases of genital loss, involves meticulous microsurgery and multidisciplinary teams to restore urinary and sexual
function, although it remains limited by surgical complexity and immunosuppression requirements. [9]


CONCLUSIONS

This systematic review demonstrates limited but diverse published evidence regarding plastic surgery involvement in complex
urology reconstruction following oncologic resections. The findings
suggest significant benefits of interdisciplinary collaboration in
complex cases requiring specialized reconstructive expertise, with
one study demonstrating statistically significant improvement in outcomes through systematic implementation of multidisciplinary care.
Further research, including larger retrospective and prospective studies, is needed to establish best practices for both patient outcomes
and cost effectiveness.
