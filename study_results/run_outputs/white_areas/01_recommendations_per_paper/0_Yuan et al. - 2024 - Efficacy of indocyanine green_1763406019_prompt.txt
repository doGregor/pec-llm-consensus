Introduction

Lymph metastasis accounts for increased disease-related
mortality and poor survival in patients with penile cancer [1].


Regardless of therapeutic efforts, the prognosis remains
unsatisfactory in patients with positive pelvic lymph nodes
(LNs) or distant metastasis [2]. Considering that the disease
progresses in a stepwise metastatic pattern such that inguinal

wileyonlinelibrary.com Published by John Wiley & Sons Ltd. www.bjui.org

LN (ILN) metastasis usually occurs first, followed by
metastases of the pelvic LNs and distant organs, early
diagnosis, and prompt treatment of ILNs remains important
for patients with penile cancer [3]. Surgical staging of ILNs is
important in the management of penile cancer and there are
different modalities for achieving it, which include videoendoscopic or open modified ILN dissection (ILND) and
dynamic sentinel LN sampling.

Accurate surgical staging of ILNs aids comprehensive
evaluation and treatment and is recommended for selected

patients with penile cancer (T1b-T4N0 or N1–3) [4].
Moreover, increasing the number of dissected ILNs might
contribute to improved survival outcomes in patients who
receive an ILND [5]. However, the possibility of
complications after ILND is generally high, which may

’
worsen patients quality of life and overall survival [6].
Therefore, many patients, including surgeons, are inclined to
refuse or delay the procedure, which may result in erroneous
assessment and disease progression. In recent years, surgical
techniques such as video-endoscopic and robot-assisted
laparoscopic ILND have been optimised to promote surgical
safety and reduce morbidity [7]. In addition, the extent of the
LND is curtailed, and the saphenous vein and some fascia
can be preserved in the modified ILND. The modified ILND
is performed as a staging or prophylactic surgery that can not
only achieve effective dissection of the ILNs but also reduce
surgical complications [8].

Dynamic sentinel LN sampling may also be recommended for
the surgical staging of the ILNs with the consideration of
minimising complications and avoiding the resection
of unnecessary ILNs. Compared to ILND, it appears to be
less invasive and associated with fewer complications. In
high-volume and centralised penile cancer centres dynamic
sentinel LN sampling is highly effective in clinically LNnegative patients. However, concerns of high false-negative
rates and limited technique availability in some centres
should be emphasised when choosing the management
approach for ILNs. Generally, dynamic sentinel LN sampling
is rarely performed on patients with penile cancer in China,
as it is unavailable in many centres and few urologists have
experience using this technique.

A good balance between improving the clinical efficacy of
ILND and reducing the risk of surgical complications is
required for individual treatment. Indocyanine green (ICG)
fluorescence imaging has been applied in LND for many
malignancies, and it achieves distinct detection of LNs and
precise removal of lesions [9]. However, the role of ICG
fluorescence-guided ILND in patients with penile cancer
remains unclear. Hence, we conducted this multicentre
randomised controlled trial (RCT) to investigate the efficacy
and safety of ICG fluorescence-guided ILND for patients with
penile cancer. This study may establish the positive role of

Patients and Methods

Study Design and Participants

This was a multicentre RCT registered in the Chinese Clinical
[Trial Registry (identifier: ChiCTR2100044584; https://www.](https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.aspx?proj=121913)
[chictr.org.cn/showproj.aspx?proj=121913). The trial protocol](https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.aspx?proj=121913)
was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board
of The Third Xiangya Hospital of Central South University
(R21061). Written informed consent was obtained from all
patients. This study was conducted in accordance with the
Declaration of Helsinki and the Clinical Trials Act. In
addition, this study was performed according to the
Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT)
guidelines.

We enrolled patients with penile cancer who underwent
bilateral ILND from 1 April 2021 to 31 June 2022 at the
Department of Urology at four centres: The Third Xiangya
Hospital of Central South University, The Second
Xiangya Hospital of Central South University, Hunan Cancer
Hospital, and Hunan Provincial People’s Hospital. The

–
inclusion criteria were as follows: aged 18 80 years at the
time of enrolment; diagnosed squamous cell carcinoma
confirmed by pathological examination (pT1b–4); clinically
staged cN0M0; no indication for radical penectomy;
indication for ILND; no history of inguinal and pelvic
surgery, chemotherapy or radiotherapy; no contraindication
for surgery; and no allergy to ICG or iodine.

Randomisation and Masking

The study used a paired design in which each patient was
randomised to ICG fluorescence-guided ILND in one groin
(ICG side) and non-ICG fluorescence-guided ILND in the
other groin (non-ICG side). Randomisation was achieved by a
single researcher using on-line software without stratification.
This study used a single-blind design. The randomised results
were open to the surgeons but masked to the patients until
the end of the study.

Procedures

All patients underwent surgical resection of the penile tumour
for pathological evaluation and recovered properly before
undergoing ILND. Patients underwent bilateral modified
ILND through a hypogastric subcutaneous approach;
however, the surgical technique (either laparoscopic or robotassisted laparoscopic ILND) was decided upon based on the
patients’ willingness. ICG (2.5 mg/mL) was dissolved in water
for injection (Eisai China Inc., Shanghai, China) and prepared
on the day of surgery. Afterward, 5 mL ICG was

subcutaneously injected through the root of the penis at
multiple points 15 min before dissection of the ILNs. A
fluorescence surgical system equipped with the fluorescence
mode was used in the surgical procedure, wherein ICG
fluorescence imaging was obtained by changing the mode
from visible light to near-infrared fluorescence. The
fluorescence mode was turned on for the ICG groin and
turned off for the non-ICG groin.

Previous studies have described the surgical procedures for
ILND in detail [10,11]. The anatomical boundaries of
modified ILND in the present study were specified as follows:
superior to 2 cm above the inguinal ligament, lateral to the
lateral border of the femoral artery (without reaching to
the sartorius muscle), and medial to the adductor longus
muscle. In addition, the greater saphenous vein was
preserved. During dissection, the superficial and deep ILNs
were removed and placed separately. Superficial ILNs were
further divided into five subgroups (central, superolateral,
inferolateral, superomedial, and inferomedial), according to
their anatomical regions [12]. At the end of the surgery, the
researchers re-checked the fluorescence status of all dissected
ILNs, which were imaged using fluorescence in vitro.
Afterward, the ILNs in each group were separately labelled
and bagged, followed by the pathological examinations.
Frozen sections were not involved, and patients were to
receive radical ILND or pelvic LND on another day if
necessitated by the pathological results. A radical ILND or
pelvic LND was performed without ICG fluorescence
imaging, and the number of dissected ILNs in the radical
ILND were not compared between the two groins.

The follow-up schedule lasted for 6 months, with three visits
at the first-, third- and sixth-month after surgery. All patients
scored their personal satisfaction with aesthetic appearance
and sensation in each groin using a visual analogue scale
(VAS) score at the last follow-up visit. Two items (aesthetic
appearance and sensory function) were rated on a scale of
score 0–10, with a score of 0 being ‘least satisfied’ and 10
being ‘most satisfied’.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome measure was the total number of ILNs
retrieved. Secondary outcome measures included
complications and the rate of LN non-compliance. LN noncompliance was defined as the inadequate removal of ILNs.
ILNs from six groups (one deep and five superficial groups)
were dissected, and pathological examination was performed
on both sides. Hence, the LN non-compliance rate was
determined by dividing the number of dissected groups
without LNs by the total number of dissected
groups (n = six). Baseline characteristics and
clinicopathological items were recorded and evaluated.
Human papillomavirus status was detected by polymerase

items (operative time, estimated blood loss, drainage volume,
in situ drain duration, unpreserved branches of the great
saphenous vein, the number of positive LNs, VAS scores of
groin aesthetic appearance and sensory function, and
recurrence and metastasis in inguinal regions) were recorded
from unilateral dissection of ILNs in one groin. Meanwhile,
items including postoperative hospital stay, pathological stage
of ILNs, follow-up time, distant metastasis, and survival were
described based on the patient’s condition. The complications

the groins or lower extremities were compared between the

Statistical Analysis

The sample size was estimated, and ~40 patients were
required for enrolment. Based on the preliminary results of
our previous study, the number of ILNs retrieved from ICG
fluorescence-guided and conventional unilateral ILND was
14.75 and 12.5 (SD 2.825), respectively. A two-sided error
probability (a level) of 0.05 and the power (1-b) of 0.9 were
set with sampling ratio of 1. The sample size was calculated
as 34; finally, at least 38 participants were needed for this
trial with the consideration of 10% loss to follow-up.

A per protocol (PP) analysis was applied in this study to
patients who completed the entire study. Meanwhile, an
intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was conducted for all
patients who underwent randomisation. Data analysis was
conducted using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences
(SPSS�) software, version 24.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY,
USA). Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s
chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. Continuous variables
were analysed by paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
The difference in total number of ILNs retrieved on the ICG
vs non-ICG side was reported with a 95% CI. All tests were
two-sided and a P < 0.05 was considered to indicate
statistical significance.

Results

In this study, 45 patients were randomised. One patient was
lost to follow-up after discharge. Therefore, for this patient,
only demographic characteristics and clinical outcomes
during the hospitalisation were available. Two patients
dropped out because of a failure of ICG fluorescence, but
they still received ILND without ICG fluorescence imaging.
No intraoperative complications or death occurred in any of
the patients. There were no complications associated with the
ICG injections. In all, 45 patients who were randomised in
this trial were included in the ITT analysis, and 42 who
completed the entire study were included in the PP analysis.

Discussion


Thorough and meticulous ILND serves as an important
procedure for staging, planning, treatment, and prognosis,
and plays a significant role in the management of penile

cancer [13]. A comprehensive assessment of ILNs can be
achieved by the pathological evaluation of laterality, location,
number, metastasis, and extranodal extension after ILND. It
has been verified that the survival rate reaches as high as
>95% in patients with pN0 staged penile cancer, whereas the
survival rate is unsatisfactory among patients with regional
LN metastasis in penile cancer [14].

Inconsistency between the clinical and pathological stage of
ILNs inevitably misleads the judgement of regional LN status.
Approximately 24% of occult ILN metastases were detected in
men with cN0 penile cancer [15]. Early excision of clinically
occult ILN metastases led to the improvement in 3-year
cancer-specific survival from 35% to 84% when compared
with delayed excision [16]. Moreover, an increased number of
retrieved ILNs was associated with improved survival in
patients with penile cancer [17]. The 5-year survival rate
could be improved if more than eight ILNs were harvested
during a unilateral ILND [18]. A higher ILN yield with ILND
led to improved overall survival, which was possibly
associated with the removal of micrometastases [19]. And the
probability of accurate LN staging was increased by a higher
LN yield that might optimise the treatment decision and
generate better outcomes, which could be described as a ‘Will
Rogers Phenomenon’ [20]. Hence, the role of surgical staging
in the management of penile cancer cannot be
underestimated.

Dynamic sentinel LN biopsy is a surgical staging technique
with a low complication rate for patients with penile cancer.
And it has been found that ICG fluorescence imaging


contributed to improve intraoperative optical identification of
sentinel LNs [21]. Additionally, the application of hybrid
ICG-99mTc-nanocolloid during dynamic sentinel LN biopsy
is safe and reliable, which could increase the detection rate of
sentinel LNs [22]. However, the disadvantages of false
negatives, reported at a higher rate in some parts of the
world, and inadequate removal of LNs may lead to
misdiagnoses [23].

An ILND using the hypogastric subcutaneous approach is a
routine procedure in the four centres, and all surgeons had
extensive experience with the procedure. We formulated the
project on the site of ICG injection, ICG dosage, waiting time
for observation, repeated administration of ICG, and
meticulous dissociation of lymphatic vessels to improve the
successful development of ILNs as much as possible.
However, when the lymphatic vessels were obstructed by
tumours or squeezed by enlarged ILNs, ICG could not access
positive ILNs. The effect of ICG fluorescence imaging on
distinguishing malignant from normal LNs is still not optimal
and was not explored in this study.

Generally, all fatty and nodal tissues are usually removed en
bloc to ensure that all LN tissues can be removed in the
standard practice of completing a modified or radical ILND.
However, a good balance between efficacy (improving the
number of LNs dissected) and safety (reducing the excision of
non-lymphocytic tissues that might contribute to decreased
complications) is momentous especially in clinically LNnegative patients. In this study, either ICG or non-ICG

Researchers have increasingly attempted to optimise this
procedure by reducing surgical complications [24]. Previous
studies have shown a reduced rate of surgical complications
after laparoscopic or robot-assisted ILND compared with that
of open ILND [25,26]. The attractive features of threedimensional views, precise identification and flexibility benefit
the reduction of tissue and vascular damage in robot-assisted
ILND [27]. Effective dissection using ICG fluorescence
imaging could minimise the damage in the groin. Based on
the results of this study, ICG was safe for application in
patients. Furthermore, we confirmed that the complication
rate of ICG fluorescence-guided ILND was similar to that of
conventional surgery. ICG fluorescence-guided ILND did not
affect inguinal sensory function or aesthetic appearance
compared with the conventional surgery. Other studies have

explored the effect of ICG fluorescence-guided surgery in
LND during prostatectomy, gastrectomy, and gynaecological
surgery for malignancies, demonstrating that ICG
fluorescence-guided LND is safe and feasible, can contribute
to identification of LNs, and may be useful intraoperatively
for removing all necessary LNs [28–30]. It has been found
that during D2 LND for the patients with gastric cancer, ICG
fluorescence imaging improved the number of LNs dissected
and reduced the LN non-compliance rate.

Although the sample size was limited owing to the rarity of
penile cancer, a multicentre RCT evaluating ICG
fluorescence-guided ILND in patients with penile caner is
significant in clinical practice and technological innovation. In
addition, the total number of ILNs harvested was compared
between the two groins of the patients. Using the paired
design with patients acting as their own control and
conducting bilateral ILND on one patient by the same
surgeon, the risk of the results being affected by differences in
baseline characteristics, surgeons’ skills, and surgical approach
could be reduced. However, the observation and comparison
of oncological outcomes and survival were not available in
the present study design. Besides that, this study had other
limitations. A large sample size and long-term follow-up are
needed to strengthen the evidence. And the value of increased
number of ILNs harvested on survival should be further
evaluated. We hope that there will be more high-quality
studies on ILND for patients with penile cancer in the future,
and that the use of ICG imaging will make a difference in
this procedure.

Conclusion

The ICG fluorescence-guided technique significantly
improved the efficacy of ILND in patients with penile cancer.
This study demonstrates that ICG fluorescence-guided ILND
in patients with penile cancer is safe and beneficial to
efficiently retrieve more ILNs. In addition, the procedure can
effectively reduce the LN non-compliance rate without
increasing the risk of surgical complications.
