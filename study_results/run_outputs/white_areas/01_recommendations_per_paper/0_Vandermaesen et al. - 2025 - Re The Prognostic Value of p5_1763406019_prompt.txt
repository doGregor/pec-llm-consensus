Status in Penile Cancer: A Systematic Review and Metaanalysis

Sand FL, Lindquist S, Aalborg GL, Kjaer SK

Pathology 2025;57:276–84

Experts’ summary:
The meta-analysis by Sand et al investigated the prognostic
relevance of p53 and Ki-67 immunohistochemistry (IHC) in
penile squamous cell carcinoma (PSCC). Overall survival
(OS) and cancer-specific survival (CSS) were analyzed using
pooled hazard ratios (HRs) and restricted mean survival
time (RMST). The authors included 20 studies, of which 15
assessed survival according to p53 status and seven according to Ki-67 status. A p53 mutant pattern was associated
with significantly worse OS (HR 2.42, 95% confidence interval (CI) 1.75–3.34) among 930 men. For CSS, assessed in 391
men, the association was even stronger (HR 4.18, 95% CI
1.87–9.35). The RMST results support this, with a mean
OS reduction of more than 10 months in the p53-mutated
group. By contrast, evidence regarding Ki-67 was limited,
but high Ki-67 expression appeared to be linked to worse
CSS (HR 1.96, 95% CI 1.15–3.32). No association was found
between OS and Ki-67. These results position p53 as a
potentially valuable biomarker in clinical decision-making
and follow-up planning in PSCC.

Experts’ comments:
Although rare, PSCC remains a challenging malignancy,
with considerable prognostic heterogeneity and no reliable
biomarkers to guide treatment and follow-up. Previous
studies have focused mainly on human papillomavirus
(HPV) status and its surrogate marker p16 [1]. However,
their prognostic value and therapeutic implications remain
uncertain. By contrast, this meta-analysis highlights P53
mutations as a more powerful prognostic factor than p16

[1]. Recent molecular studies support the relevance of p53
in PSCC: a single-cell RNA sequencing analysis showed a
highly aggressive, immune-excluded tumor microenvironment in TP53-mutated PSCC [2]. The higher prevalence of
TP53 mutations in HPV-independent PSCC may explain the
poorer prognosis in comparison to HPV-associated tumors,
possibly because of greater therapy resistance in advanced
TP53-mutated cases.

Nonetheless, key challenges remain before p53 IHC can
be clinically implemented, particularly regarding its interpretation. Recent evidence supports a more accurate pattern-based framework [3]. Hence, the results from this
meta-analysis should be interpreted with caution, as null
patterns and cytoplasmic overexpression of p53 on IHC
were disregarded in several of the studies included. Standardized interpretation will be crucial to ensure reproducibility across studies and clinical settings. Another
challenge is integration of p53 with p16 IHC in clinical practice and the definition of prognostic subgroups. One widely
used classification, as applied in vulvar cancer, categorizes
cases as HPV-associated, HPV-independent/p53-wildtype,
and HPV-independent/p53-mutant tumors [4]. However,
p53 mutations also occur in HPV-associated PSCC cases,
which complicates subgroup definitions [5].
Taken together, these findings underscore the growing
potential of p53 as a clinically meaningful prognosticator
in PSCC. However, widespread implementation will depend
on further prospective validation and standardized IHC
interpretation.
