Based on the provided paper, which focuses exclusively on the treatment of **Penile Metastasis (PM)**—cancer that has spread *to* the penis from a primary tumor elsewhere—and not on primary penile cancer (e.g., stages Tis, T1, T2), the following treatment recommendations and approaches are identified.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General Approach to Care** | **Specific Clinical Indications:** Patients with Penile Metastasis (PM).<br>**Recommendation:** A personalized treatment plan should be developed by a multidisciplinary team to determine the best option for the individual patient. |
| **Systemic & Surgical Therapies** | **Specific Clinical Indications:** Penile Metastasis (PM).<br>**Recommendation:** Surgery and chemotherapy are common treatment approaches for managing penile metastases. |
| **Radiotherapy (General Palliative Use)** | **Specific Clinical Indications:** Symptomatic Penile Metastasis (PM).<br>**Recommendation:** Radiotherapy is primarily used in a palliative setting to manage symptoms such as pain, priapism, bleeding, and urinary dysfunction. Conventional palliative dosages typically range from 25 Gy to 42 Gy administered in 10 to 14 fractions. |
| **Radiotherapy (Higher-Dose for Local Control)** | **Specific Clinical Indications:** Penile Metastasis (PM) where more durable local control or a curative-intent approach is considered.<br>**Recommendation:** Higher total radiation doses, up to 56-60 Gy using conventional fractionation (e.g., in 30 fractions), may be administered for better symptom control. One report noted a curative attempt using 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions with concurrent capecitabine. |
| **Radiotherapy (Adjuvant)** | **Specific Clinical Indications:** Post-surgical management of Penile Metastasis (PM).<br>**Recommendation:** Adjuvant radiotherapy (e.g., a regimen of 30 Gy in 10 fractions) can be considered after surgery to help prevent recurrence. |
| **Stereotactic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT)** | **Specific Clinical Indications:** Penile Metastasis (PM), particularly in cases of oligometastatic disease.<br>**Recommendation:** Stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) is a valuable approach that can provide good symptomatic relief and local control. It may be considered an alternative to surgery and can potentially offer a curative or prolonged local control outcome.<br>• A specific protocol of **25 Gy in 5 fractions** was reported as feasible and effective in the paper's case study. |
