Lymph node (LN) metastasis is the major prognostic factor
for survival of PeCA. [5] Accurate assessment of regional LNs is essential for proper management because resection of small-volume,
pathologically involved regional LNs can be curative. [6] Inguinal
and pelvic LNs consist of regional LNs of the penis. Thus, therapeutic radical inguinal LN dissection and pelvic LN dissection are important treatments for PeCA. [7]
18 F-FDG PET or PET/CT has been reported to be functional
and useful imaging modalities for tumor staging in different cancers. [8] Also, [18] F-FDG PET/CT had an improved diagnostic accuracy
and is now considered an important technique in cancer imaging. [9]
A meta-analysis showed that [18] F-FDG PET/CT imaging has relatively low sensitivity for detection of inguinal LN involvement in
PeCA patients, which does not justify its routine application in
PeCA, especially true for clinically node-negative patients. [10] They
also concluded that patients with clinically palpable LNs may benefit from [18] F-FDG PET/CT because the sensitivity in this subgroup
of patients is fairly high. [10] However, this meta-analysis did not evaluated the diagnostic test accuracy of [18] F-FDG PET/CT for pelvic
LN staging in PeCA patients.
The purpose of the current study is to meta-analyze the published data on the diagnostic performance of [18] F-FDG PET/CT for detection of LN metastasis PeCA, in order to provide more evidencebased data and to address further studies in the evaluation of diagnostic
value of [18] F-FDG PET/CT for LN staging in PeCA patients.

PATIENTS AND METHODS

We used the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses statement to improve the reporting of
our research. [11]

Data Sources and Search Strategy
A structured approach was followed to identify the patient
PICOS (population, interventions, comparators, outcomes, and
study design) criteria. [11] We conducted electronic English-language
literature searches of PubMed, Cochrane database, and Embase from
the earliest available date of indexing through August 31, 2021. We
also hand-searched the reference lists of identified publications for
additional studies. The search strategy included both subject headings
(MeSH terms) and keywords for the target condition (PeCA), the imaging techniques under investigation ( [18] F-FDG PET/CT), and the interventions (LN staging). We used a search algorithm based on a
combination of terms: (1) “PET” OR “positron emission tomography” OR “positron emission tomography/computed tomography”
OR “PET/CT” OR “positron emission tomography-computed tomography” OR “PET-CT” AND (2) “Penile Neoplasms” OR “Penile
cancer” OR “Penile carcinoma” AND (3) “Lymph node” AND (4)
“Metastasis” OR “Staging.”

Traditional anatomical imaging modalities, such as CT and/

of low specificity. [35] MRI may not provide significant additional information over CT for detection of inguinal LN metastasis, but MRI

LN metastasis in 7 patients with PeCA. [37]
The current meta-analysis showed a good sensitivity and
specificity of [18] F-FDG PET/CT for the detection of inguinal and
pelvic LN metastasis in PeCA patients. Previous meta-analysis
demonstrated that [18] F-FDG PET/CT imaging has relatively low
sensitivity for detection of inguinal LN involvement in PeCA patients. [10] However, they only evaluated the diagnostic performances
of [18] F-FDG PET/CT for inguinal LN staging in PeCA patients. The
current study simultaneously investigated the diagnostic performances of [18] F-FDG PET/CT for detection of inguinal and pelvic


LN metastasis in PeCA patients, resulting in similar sensitivity
and specificity. Also, comparison of AUC of [18] F-FDG PET/CT
for detection of inguinal and pelvic LN metastasis shows no
statistical differences in the current study. Graafland et al [22]
found a sensitivity of 91% and specificity of 100% for pelvic
LN spread and concluded that [18] F-FDG PET/CT scanning appears promising for detecting pelvic LN metastases with great
accuracy, and it identifies distant metastases in PeCA patients
with inguinal LN metastasis. Ottenhof et al [26] investigated diagnostic value for pelvic and distant staging in patients with high-risk 61
PeCA patients. In their study, for staging of pelvic LNs, sensitivity
was 85%, specificity was 75%, negative predictive value was 90%,
and positive predictive value was 65%. [26] For the detection of distant
metastases, [18] F-FDG PET/CT had a positive predictive value
of 93%. [26]
The current meta-analysis showed a considerable heterogeneity of sensitivity and specificity between studies. The included studies were statistically heterogeneous in their estimates of sensitivity
and specificity. This heterogeneity is likely to arise through diversity in methodological aspects between different studies, and the basic differences among the patients in the included studies may have
contributed to the observed heterogeneity of the results too. To minimize bias in the selection of studies and in the data extraction, reviewers who were blinded to the journal, author, institution, and
date of publication independently selected articles based on the inclusion criteria, and scores were assigned to study design characteristics and examination results by using a standardized form that was
based on the QUADAS-2 tool.
