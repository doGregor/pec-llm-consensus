Based on the systematic review provided, the following explicit treatment recommendations for penile cancer are extracted and organized as requested.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Treatment** | **Penile-Preserving Surgery (PPS):** Should be performed wherever possible as it is the standard treatment to preserve penile length and function without compromising oncological outcomes. |
| **Primary Treatment** | **Glansectomy:** Is the indicated treatment for invasive penile cancer (PeCa) confined to the glans penis, including lesions invading the glans spongiosum (T2). |
| **Primary Treatment / Surgical Technique** | **Glansectomy with Distal Corporectomy:** For tumors extending into the distal corporal tips, a distal corporectomy should be performed in addition to glansectomy. |
| **Surgical Technique** | **Surgical Margins:** A resection margin of less than 5 mm is acceptable for achieving oncological control in penile-preserving surgery (PPS). |
| **Surgical Technique** | **Intraoperative Margin Assessment:** Frozen section analysis of proximal margins should be considered during surgery to reduce the risk of positive margins, local recurrence, and subsequent penectomy. |
| **Surgical Technique / Staging** | **Intraoperative Biopsies:** Following glansectomy, deep separate tunical biopsies should be obtained from the corpora to ensure an accurate pathological assessment of the deep surgical margins. |
| **Diagnosis / Staging** | **Preoperative Imaging:** Formal staging with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is important before surgery to distinguish between T2 (spongiosum invasion) and T3 (cavernosa involvement) disease. This helps in selecting the correct patients for glansectomy alone versus glansectomy with distal corporectomy. |
| **Salvage Treatment** | **Salvage Penectomy:** Patients who experience a local recurrence or are found to have positive surgical margins after initial organ-sparing surgery can be offered a salvage penectomy. |
| **Follow-up Care** | **Strict Follow-up:** A strict follow-up protocol is necessary after penile-preserving surgery (PPS) due to a higher incidence of local recurrence compared to more radical procedures. |
| **Patient Selection & Follow-up Care** | **Patient Education and Compliance:** Before performing PPS, clinicians must ensure that patients are capable of and will commit to regular self-examination and attending scheduled follow-up appointments. |
| **Setting of Care** | **Referral to Specialist Centers:** As penile cancer is a rare disease, surgery should be performed in high-volume specialist centers by experienced surgeons to optimize outcomes. |
