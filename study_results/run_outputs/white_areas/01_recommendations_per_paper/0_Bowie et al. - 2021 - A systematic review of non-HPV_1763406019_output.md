Based on the systematic review provided, the following treatment recommendations and clinical strategies are identified:

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **For Detected Metastatic Disease:** The detection of metastatic inguinal lymph nodes requires surgical resection by inguinal lymphadenectomy to limit the spread of disease. <br><br> *Additional Clinical Clarification:* This applies to patients with confirmed lymph node metastasis. |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **For High-Risk, Clinically Node-Negative Disease (Proposed Standard of Care):** Offer prophylactic inguinal lymphadenectomy to all but the lowest risk groups. <br><br> *Specific Clinical Indications:* Patients with penile cancer classified as ≥pT1G2 with cN0 disease. <br><br> *Additional Clinical Clarification:* This strategy is for patients with clinically non-palpable nodes (cN0) to address potential micrometastatic disease. The paper notes this approach leads to significant overtreatment and morbidity. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Biomarker-Informed Risk Stratification)** | **For Clinically Node-Negative Disease (Biomarker-Based Recommendation):** Consider using p53 overexpression to identify patients at higher risk of Lymph Node Metastasis (LNM) who should be considered for early inguinal lymphadenectomy. <br><br> *Specific Clinical Indications:* Patients within the clinically node-negative (cN0) subgroup who are found to have p53 overexpression. <br><br> *Additional Clinical Clarification:* The paper suggests this may be a useful adjunct to aid decision-making for the difficult-to-treat clinically node-negative subgroup, helping to stratify risk and select patients for lymphadenectomy. |
