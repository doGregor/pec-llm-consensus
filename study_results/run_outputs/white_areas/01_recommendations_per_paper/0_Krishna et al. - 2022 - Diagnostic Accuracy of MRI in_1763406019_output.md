| **Lymph Node Management** | **For patients with ≥T2 disease:** Prophylactic inguinal lymph node dissection or dynamic sentinel node biopsy may be warranted, even in cases with non-palpable lymph nodes. This is in contrast to staged or delayed lymph node dissection. |
| **Primary Treatment** | **For patients with T3 disease (invasion of corpora cavernosa):** Organ-sparing surgical approaches are usually precluded. Complete or partial penectomy is warranted, with the specific choice depending on the tumor's location. |
| **Diagnosis / Staging** | **For preoperative evaluation when organ-sparing surgery is planned:** MRI staging is recommended for all patients in this category to assess for T3 disease. The high specificity of MRI in detecting T3 disease makes it particularly useful for this purpose. |
| **Diagnosis / Staging** | **For distinguishing ≤T1 from ≥T2 disease:** MRI staging may be considered to complement clinical staging, or as an alternative when clinical assessment is equivocal or challenging. |
| **Diagnosis / Staging** | **For routine preoperative evaluation:** MRI may be valuable for the local staging of penile cancer, especially when organ-sparing treatment options are being considered. |
| **Diagnosis / Staging (Imaging Protocol)** | **When performing preoperative local staging MRI:** Institutions may choose to omit artificial erection (induced by intracavernosal injection of prostaglandin E1) from their MRI protocols, as it has been shown to have similar accuracy to MRI without artificial erection and adds extra time, expense, and potential complications. |
