Abstract

Penile intraepithelial neoplasia (PeIN) is a rare skin condition with potential to progress
to invasive penile cancer. We performed a systematic review of treatment options and
outcomes for PeIN. Topical agents showed response and recurrence rates of 40–100% and
20% for imiquimod, and 48–74% and 11% for 5-fluorouracil, respectively. Discontinuation
of topical agents because of side effects was observed in 12% of cases. Response rates for
laser therapies were 52–100%, with recurrence in 7–48% of cases and a change in penile
sensitivity in 50%. Circumcision cleared preputial PeIN. Rates of recurrence after surgical
treatment of glans PeIN were 25% for wide local excision, 4% for Mohs surgery, 5% for
total glans resurfacing, and 10% for glansectomy. There are limited data on factors
predictive of treatment response and on sequencing of treatment options.
Patient summary: Several treatment options are available for men with precancerous
lesions of the foreskin or glans. Close follow-up is necessary as lesions can recur or
progress to invasive penile cancer.

We performed a systematic review on April 9, 2020 to
summarise treatment options and outcomes in the literature using MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Database of
Systematic Reviews. A broad search using several combinations, synonyms, and search terms related to PIN, laser/
phototherapy, topical, and surgical treatment options, as
well as HPV vaccination, was performed (Supplementary
material). Non-English literature was excluded. The reference lists of the publications identified were manually
screened to identify further studies. Duplicate articles were


filtered using the “close match function” in Endnote and
manual deduplication. All titles, abstracts, and full texts
were screened for appropriateness for inclusion by two
investigators using Covidence [(www.covidence.org/).](http://www.covidence.org/) Data
from the same studies that appeared in multiple publications were only considered once. Only published manuscripts for cohorts with a minimum of ten men with PeIN
were included. Any discrepancies were resolved by a third
investigator (C.D.F.).
Of 1594 publications, 94 full-text manuscripts were
screened. Nineteen studies involving 691 patients with PeIN
were included (Table 1). Imiquimod and 5-fluorouacil (5FU) were used in three studies each, laser treatment in nine,

including YAG lasers in five, CO 2 lasers in one, unspecified
lasers in three, and phototherapy in three. Glans resurfacing
and wide local excision were described in three and two

studies, respectively. One study described wide local excision followed by laser therapy. Glansectomy was described
in one report. Response to second-line treatment after
failure of first-line therapy options was not reported in
any of the studies.
Topical therapies included imiquimod and 5-FU, with
complete response rates in 40–100% and 48–74% of cases,
respectively. Progression after imiquimod and 5-FU was
observed in 20% and 11%, respectively. Adverse events were
observed in 10% during imiquimod and in 5–12% during 5FU treatment; inflammation causing pain and severe irritation were the most common adverse events. Phimosis was

observed in 5% of patients treated with imiquimod. Discontinuation of treatment by men treated with imiquimod and
5-FU was described in 10% and 11% of cases, respectively,
with one study reporting that 11% of men treated with 5-FU
required admission for severe inflammation.
Energy-based therapies discussed in the literature
included YAG and CO 2 lasers and phototherapy. Laser treatment had total response rates of 52–100%. Recurrence was
reported in 7–48% of cases. Altered penile sensitivity following laser treatment was described, including increased
sensitivity in 50% and decreased sensitivity in 15% of cases.
Phototherapy led to complete response in 40–70% of cases,
with recurrence observed in 30% and progression in 36%.
Surgical treatment of PeIN was associated with recurrence in 4–30% of cases. Circumcision cleared all preputial
PeIN, whereas recurrence after surgical treatment of PeIN of
the glans was observed in 25% of cases after wide local
excision, 4% after Mohs surgery, 5% after total glans resurfacing, and 10% after glansectomy.
This review shows that the literature for treatment

options and outcomes for men with PeIN is limited and
based on small retrospective cohort studies, but several
conclusions can be drawn. The preferred treatment for
preputial PeIN is circumcision, which may decrease the
toxicity of topical treatment of PeIN of the glans.
In our experience, some men with PeIN of the foreskin
and glans may show complete response of the glans PeIN
after circumcision alone. It could be hypothesised that this
relates to improved hygiene and changes in the microenvironment following removal of the foreskin. However, no
evidence in the literature shows this.

In cases of PeIN of the glans, organ-sparing approaches
including topical or laser treatments should be recommended as first-line options given their efficacy and relatively low levels of recurrence and progression. Data
describing the efficacy of further lines of treatment after
failure of first-line options are limited. Most clinicians
would offer patients a second topical or laser treatment.
Men with extensive PeIN or recurrence after first- and

second-line topical and/or laser treatment should be offered
surgical options including wide local excision or glans
resurfacing. Given a considerable risk of recurrence for all
treatment options, close follow-up for men with PeIN is
essential.


HPV is an insufficiently studied risk factor and potential
treatment target. It is well documented that HPV infection is a
causative agent in malignancies including oropharyngeal [3],
anal [2], and penile cancers [5]. In a prospective cohort of
3033menfromtheUSA, Brazil,and Mexico,HPVwasdetected
in 1788/3033 (59%), of whom 10/1788 (0.5%) developed PeIN
during 12-mo follow-up [5]. In studies evaluating the efficacy
of HPV vaccination, a significant reduction in cervical cancer
risk was noted [6]. The efficacy of HPV vaccination as primary
prophylaxis has been demonstrated for adolescents, but secondary prophylaxis among men with PeIN or invasive penile
cancer remains to be studied [7].
Our search criteria were designed and reviewed by experienced clinicians and predefined in a peer-reviewed protocol. It is possible that not all of the potentially relevant
studies were identified because of undetected imprecision
in the search strategy, which is a potential source of bias.
The retrospective nature of the review and the limited
number of publications on the topic, mostly single-centre
studies with small case numbers, combined with the differing treatment course lengths and follow-up protocols, may
also influence bias.

In summary, the data on treatment options and outcomes for men with PeIN are limited and from small retro
spective cohort studies. The preferred treatment for preputial PeIN is circumcision, whilst first-line treatment for
glans disease remains topical therapy.
