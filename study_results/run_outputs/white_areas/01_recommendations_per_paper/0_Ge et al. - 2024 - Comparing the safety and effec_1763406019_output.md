Based on the provided paper, here are the explicit treatment recommendations for penile cancer, presented in the specified format.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Systemic Therapy (Adjuvant)** | Adjuvant radiotherapy is recommended for patients with pN3 squamous penile cancer to improve oncological outcomes. |
| **Systemic Therapy (Neoadjuvant)** | For patients with enlarged inguinal lymph nodes or late-stage disease with large nodes, neoadjuvant chemotherapy is recommended to reduce the tumor burden before surgery. A recommended regimen is triple therapy including cisplatin and taxane. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Confirmed Metastases)** | Patients with confirmed lymphatic metastases are recommended to undergo radical lymphadenectomy and adjuvant chemotherapy. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Staging for Non-Palpable Nodes)** | Aggressive staging is crucial for patients with non-palpable (clinically node-negative, cN0) inguinal lymph nodes. The following approaches are recommended:<br>• **Dynamic Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (DSNB):** Indicated for surgical staging in centers with available technology and expertise.<br>• **Surgical Staging (ILND):** Where DSNB is not available, surgical staging via inguinal lymph node dissection (e.g., modified O-ILND) is strongly recommended. Evidence also supports the use of minimally invasive ILND for cN0 disease. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Palpable Nodes / Lymphadenopathy)** | For patients with palpable inguinal nodes or inguinal lymphadenopathy, radical Open Inguinal Lymph Node Dissection (O-ILND) remains the standard approach and is recommended. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Advanced Disease)** | For patients with N2/N3 disease, lymph node metastases should be treated with radical surgery combined with adjuvant chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Recurrence)** | For patients who experience a recurrence of inguinal lymph nodes after a previous radical ILND, neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by radical lymph node surgery is recommended. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Surgical Modality for cN1-2 Disease)** | For patients with cN1-2 disease, open surgery (O-ILND) should be the preferred option over minimally invasive techniques at this time. This is to reduce the risk of large lymph node fractures during dissection. The use of minimally invasive ILND in this patient group should be conducted within the context of a clinical trial. |
| **Lymph Node Management (General Surgical Approach)** | For suitable penile cancer cases requiring inguinal lymph node dissection (e.g., cN0 disease), minimally invasive surgery (MIS) appears to be a better option than O-ILND. MIS is associated with shorter length of stay, reduced blood loss, and a lower overall complication rate, with comparable recurrence and survival rates. |
