## Comparing the safety and effectiveness of minimally invasive surgery and open inguinal lymph node dissection in penile cancer: A systematic review and meta-analysis

Si Ge [a][,][b][,][1], Lei Zheng [a][,][1], Yunxiang Li [a][,][b][,][*], Lijian Gan [a], Zuoping Wang [a], Zhiqiang Zeng [b],

**1. Introduction**


A B S T R A C T

*Objective:* To compare the safety and effectiveness of minimally invasive surgery (MIS) with open inguinal lymph
node dissection (O-ILND) in penile cancer.
*Methods:* We performed a systematic reviews and cumulative meta-analyses of primary results of interest according to PRISMA criteria, and quality assessment followed AMSTAR. The system searched five databases,
including Zhiwang, Embase, PubMed, Cochrane Library and Web of Science. The search period ranged was from
database creation until September 2023. The statistical analysis software used Stata16.
*Results:* A total of 16 studies, including 898 patients. Compared to O-ILND, MIS is superior in length of stay
(WMD = − 2.96, 95%CI [-4.38, − 1.54], P *<* 0.05), drainage time (WMD = − 3.24, 95%CI [-4.70, − 1.78], P *<*
0.05) and estimated blood loss (WMD = − 35.70, 95%CI [-46.27, − 25.14], P *<* 0.05), while operation time,
recurrence rate and 5-year overall survival rate are the same. The number of lymph nodes dissection between the
two groups are not statistically significant. Subgroup analyses found that there are more lymph nodes dissection
in robotic-assisted inguinal lymph nodes dissection (WMD = 0.50, 95%CI [0.20, 0.80], P *<* 0.05). The overall
complication rate of MIS was lower (OR = 0.26, 95%CI [0.09, 0.70], P *<* 0.05).
*Conclusion:* Minimally invasive inguinal lymph nodes dissection appears to be a better option for penile cancer
cases. But more large samples and multicenter studies are needed to further confirm.


Penile cancer is a rare disease that causes quite a bit of physical and
psychological damage. Patients with low-stage lymph node metastases
can achieve durable survival with surgery alone, but overall survival is
low in patients with extensive local metastases [1]. The incidence of the
disease shows significant differences in geographical locations [2].
There are many risk factors for penile cancer, such as lack of circumcision in children, chronic inflammation, and human oncovirus (HPV)
infection [3].
Penile cancer is dominated by lymph nodes metastasis, the presence
or absence of regional lymph nodes and the operation time for regional
lymph nodes dissection are the most important prognostic factors


affecting the survival [4,5]. A small number of lymph node metastases
can be cured by dissection, while others require multimodal treatment
with a combination of surgery, chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy. The
5-year cancer specific survival (CSS) for penile cancer without adjuvant

–
therapy is 20 34 % and up to 42 % with treatment. One study in two
centres in the UK showed that adjuvant radiotherapy improved oncological outcomes in pN3 squamous penile cancer. The study found a
5-year CSS rate of 51 % in patients who received adjuvant radiation
therapy [6]. For neoadjuvant chemotherapy for enlarged inguinal lymph
nodes, triple therapy including cisplatin and taxane is recommended,
resulting in median overall survival of 17 months [7]. Patients who
already have lymphatic metastases recommended to undergo radical
lymphadenectomy and adjuvant chemotherapy [8].

In most European high-volume centres, surgical staging of the groin
is performed using dynamic sentinel lymph node biopsy (DSNB). In
these centres DSNB is indicated for patients with cN0 disease and false
negative rates are low [9]. However, this is not the case worldwide,
where false negative rates are higher. This possibly reflects experience
and smaller case numbers. In addition, DSNB technology and equipment
may not be universally accessible. A meta-analysis showed that the
pooled sensitivity of DSNB was on 77.1 % [10]. Where DSNB is not
available, surgical staging of the cN0 groin is still strongly recommended
as it reduces mortality from penile cancer [11]. Modified open inguinal
lymph node dissection (O-ILND) techniques are usually used in this
context, modified to reduce morbidity. For palpable inguinal nodes,
O-ILND remains the standard approach [12]. However, O-ILND has a
high complication rate. Since Bishoff et al. [13] first reported the
Video-endoscopic inguinal lymph nodes dissection (VE-ILND), a variety
of minimally invasive surgery modalities have been developed [14].
Currently, MIS for ILND for penile cancer include VE-ILND, robotic-assisted inguinal lymph nodes dissection (RA-ILND) and laparoscopic
inguinal lymph nodes dissection (L-ILND) [15]. The meta-analysis by
Patel et al. [16] compared RA-ILND/VE-ILND with O-ILND without a
separate comparison and without analyzing L-ILND. Another
meta-analysis by Hu et al. [17] only compared VE-ILND and O-ILND.
None of their studies included all MIS methods and needed further
refinement. Therefore, we performed this meta-analysis to evaluate the
role of MIS for lymph node metastases in penile cancer.

**2. Methods**

*2.1. Literature search*

We performed a systematic reviews and cumulative meta-analyses of
primary outcomes of interest against PRISMA criteria [18,19] and
quality assessment following AMSTAR criteria [20]. This systematic
review has been registered with PROSPERO.
The literature search and screening process was done independently
by two researchers, and disagreements between the authors were
resolved by negotiation, with a third reviewer (YX-Li) participating in
the judgment when an agreement could not be reached. The system
searched five databases:ZhiWang, Embase, PubMed, Cochrane Library
and Web of Science. The search period ranged was from database creation until September 2023. The search terms were as follows (using
PubMed as an example): (penile cancer or penis tumor or Penis Neoplasms) AND (inguinal lymph nodes dissection or inguinal lymphadenectomy) AND (laparoscopic or robotic or video-endoscopic or
minimally invasive). Manual search of relevant research references expands the scope of the search.

*2.2. Eligibility criteria*

We included studies that included only retrospective, prospective,
and randomized controlled trials (RCTs). Inclusion criteria were (1)
Penile cancer was diagnosed and treated with inguinal lymph node
dissection; (2) The surgical approach for the same study needs to include
both of the following: Minimally invasive surgery (VE-ILND, RA-ILND,
L-ILND) and O-ILND; (3) Contain at least one outcome of interest,
including operation time (OT), length of stay (LOS), drainage time,
estimated blood loss (EBL), overall complication rate, the number of
lymph nodes dissection, recurrence rate, and 5-year overall survival
rate. Exclusion criteria were (1) The object of research is not human; (2)
There were no outcome measures of interest; (3) Research with
incomplete data or missing key data; (4) The form was editorials, conferences, expert opinions and other types of research.

*2.3. Study quality assessment*

For non-randomized controlled trials, we assessed the quality using


the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS) [21], which totals 9 points, and the
bias in cohort selection, comparability, exposure and outcomes was

– –
assessed for "low quality (0 3 points)", "medium quality (4 6 points)"

–
and "high quality (7 9 points)". For randomized controlled trials, we

–
assessed the quality using the Jadad scale. 1 3 score is low quality and
4–7 is high quality.

*2.4. Data extraction*

Two researchers independently extracted data from the study using
standardized tables, including: author, year, type, country, time, center,
sample, operation, age, quality score, aim, and outcome measures of
interest, including OT, LOS, drainage time, EBL, overall complication
rate, The number of lymph nodes dissection, recurrence rate, and 5-year
overall survival rate.

*2.5. Statistical analysis*

We used the statistical analysis software Stata 16 for data analysis.
The weighted mean difference (WMD) represents a continuous variable
and the odds ratio (OR) or relative risk (RR) represents the dichotomy,
and a 95 % confidence interval (95 % CI) was calculated. The use of chisquare tests to test the heterogeneity between included studies, such as
I [2] *>* 50 % or P *<* 0.10, indicates that the heterogeneity between studies

done according to the minimally invasive surgical approach: VE-ILND vs

**4. Discussion**

Penile cancer is a rare disease that can cause serious harm to patients,
so early diagnosis is important [37]. Penile cancer is characterized by
gradual spread, from the inguinal to the pelvic lymph nodes (PLN) and
finally far away [38]. Survival rates vary in patients with penile cancer,
with patients with low lymph nodes metastatic load surviving longer
after surgery, but patients with significant localized regional metastases
have a poorer prognosis [39]. If metastatic spread occurs, the mortality
rate of penile cancer will be very high. Therefore, the management of
inguinal lymph nodes is crucial for the prognosis of penile cancer [40].
In non-palpable lymph nodes, aggressive staging should be performed
according to the risk factors for the primary tumor. In N2/N3 disease,


lymph node metastases should be treated with radical surgery and
adjuvant chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy [51]. The use of
video-endoscopic inguinal lymph nodes dissection by Tobias-Machado
et al. [22] to treat penile cancer and its confirmation that it was superior in many ways to open surgery has led to a wave of research into the
application of minimally invasive surgery to inguinal lymph nodes
dissection in penile cancer.
We found very few meta-analyses comparing the safety and efficacy
of minimally invasive surgery versus open surgery for inguinal lymph
nodes dissection in penile cancer. A meta-analysis by Hu et al. [17]
included 10 studies comparing video-endoscopic inguinal lymph nodes
dissection with open inguinal lymph nodes dissection. The results of the
study showed that the length of stay and drainage time of
video-endoscopic inguinal lymph nodes dissection was shorter than that
of open surgery. However, the number of lymph nodes dissection was
lower than that of open surgery. Because it does not include other
minimally invasive surgical methods, it does not respond well to the
difference between minimally invasive and open surgery in patients
with penile cancer. The Patel et al. [16] study included eight studies
with small sample sizes and high heterogeneity of outcome measures,
and the accuracy of their findings needs to be further explored. So, we
did this meta-analysis to refine it further.
When analysing the duration of surgery, we found high inter-study
heterogeneity. We analyzed that these heterogeneities may be due to
operator proficiency, surgical equipment used, local medical conditions,
and regional differences. In Europe and North America, the incidence of
penile cancer is low ( *<* 1 per 100,000), In South America, Southeast Asia,

Twelve studies reported the LOS. The heterogeneity test results
showed high inter-study heterogeneity, so a random-effects model was

Eleven studies reported the drainage time. Heterogeneity test results

showed that the drainage time for MIS was shorter than that of

drainage time. We analyzed that a reduction in drainage time may be
associated with a reduction in overall complication rates. Complications
of radical ILND are closely related to lymphatic drainage and wound
healing [51].
Nine studies reported the EBL. Our meta-analysis showed that the
EBL for MIS was less than that of O-ILND (P *<* 0.05). This was similar to
previous research. Studies such as Hu et al. [17] attribute it to minimally
invasive surgery that enlarges the surgical field of vision, can clearly
identify blood vessels and give treatment.
Patients with recurrence of inguinal lymph nodes after radical ILND
have a poor prognosis, with a five-year survival rate of 16 percent [7].
There is no evidence of optimal treatment, but neoadjuvant chemotherapy and radical lymph node surgery are recommended [51]. Our
study showed no difference in recurrence rate between MIS and O-ILND

found that pN2, and pN3 had higher recurrence rates than pN0 and pN1.
Previous studies have shown that VE-ILND and RA-ILND had similar
recurrence rates to O-ILND [16,17]. The same is true of our findings.

As penile cancer progressively metastasizes from the primary tumor
to the inguinal lymph nodes, and then metastasizes to the ipsilateral
pelvic lymph nodes before extensive metastases occur. Staging and
treatment selection are based on this mode of transmission [3]. Current
imaging modalities lack sensitivity for micrometastatic disease in patients with occult inguinal lymph nodes (CN0) and primary tumor risk
characteristics. In clinically normal inguinal lymph nodes, there is a
approximately 25 % chance of developing micrometastatic disease.
Therefore, the surgical staging is critical for its subsequent treatment.
Staging must be based on lymph nodes and surgical margins. The pathology report must include histologic variation, grading, nerve and
vascular invasion, and surgical margins [51]. So, in patients with
inguinal lymphadenopathy, radical ILND is recommended [51]. For
radical ILND, the number of lymph nodes is of concern. Overall, there


was no significant difference from MIS compared to O-ILND (P *>* 0.05).
However, subgroup analyses found that RA-ILND cleared more lymph

difference in the number of lymph nodes dissection between L-ILND and

the anatomical level of the initial dissection. In the study of Master et al.

[43], They started the plane of dissection superficial to the Scarpa fascia
and obtained a higher number of lymph nodes dissection. However,
there was no difference that were dissected directly below the Scapa
fascia [44]. A study by Patel et al. [45] found that RA-ILND had more
lymph nodes dissection than O-ILND, and the results were comparable.
Studies by Abdullatif et al. [46] have found that in specific cases, the use
of da Vinci robotic systems may soon become standard of care because it
was currently the least invasive method and was associated with lower
morbidity and mortality. While RA-ILND has good prospects in terms of
lymph nodes dissection, results may be affected due to the small number
of studies included in subgroup analyses.
An increase in body mass index is an important risk factor, and
lymph node density is a prognostic factor for complications [51].

Minimally invasive surgery provides another viable surgical
approach for penile cancer patients who require lymph nodes dissection,
allowing surgeons to perform surgery for patients who would otherwise
be unsuitable or personally unwilling to undergo surgery. What’s more,
minimally invasive appears to have similar short-to medium-term
oncology outcomes to open techniques [50]. However, before performing minimally invasive inguinal lymph node dissection, the doctor
should perform a thorough evaluation. For example, the stage of the
tumor, the grade, the size of the lymph nodes, and the patient’s economic situation. When the stage is late and the lymph nodes are large,
preoperative neoadjuvant chemotherapy is usually recommended to
reduce the tumor burden before evaluation for surgery [51]. Despite
guideline testing for minimally invasive ILND in patients with cN1-2
disease as part of a clinical trial, we should opt for open surgery at
this time in order to reduce the risk of large lymph node fractures.
Current evidence supports minimally invasive ILND for cN0 disease



[12]. Due to the rarity of penile cancer and the limited number of cases,
more large samples and long-term follow-up studies are needed to
further explore the indications of minimally invasive inguinal lymph
nodes.

After a detailed quality score of the included studies, we analyzed
most of the studies as moderate. Although most of the studies were
retrospective, the research process and methods were in line with the
NOS scale. Due to the subjective factors in the quality assessment process, this may have an impact on the results of our research. In addition
to this, there are the following limitations to our study: First, we
included studies in different regions, which may have biased results due
to differences in the incidence and medical level of cancer in different

regions, resulting in a high degree of heterogeneity in our results. Second, the small sample size of some studies may also lead to heterogeneity in the results, reducing the reliability of the results; Third, the
number of studies included in the analysis was small when performing
subgroup analyses, and the reliability of the findings needed to be
further confirmed; Fourth, because the included studies rarely reported
on patients’ postoperative survival, we only analyzed the survival rate of
patients for five years after surgery. However, only three studies were
included in the analysis, so be careful when citing the results. We hope
that in the future, there will be more large-sample, multi-center studies
to further confirm our analysis results.
