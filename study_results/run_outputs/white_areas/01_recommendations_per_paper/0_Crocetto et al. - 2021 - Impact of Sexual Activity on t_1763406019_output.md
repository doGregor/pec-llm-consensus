Based on the provided systematic review, the following are the explicit recommendations related to the prevention and risk reduction of penile cancer. The paper does not contain treatment recommendations for established, stage-specific penile cancer (e.g., PeIN, Ta, T1, T2).

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Prevention / Risk Reduction** | **Circumcision:** Described as an effective strategy for penile tumor prevention. <br>• **Specific Clinical Indication:** Protection against invasive penile cancer (PC). A meta-analysis showed a strong protective effect (OR 0.33), and another study reported a hazard ratio of 0.33 for lowering PC risk. <br>• **Treatment Modality:** Childhood/adolescent circumcision. <br>• **Additional Clinical Clarification:** Circumcision is also noted as highly important for the prevention and treatment of phimosis, which is a risk factor for penile cancer. |
| **Prevention / Risk Reduction** | **HPV Vaccination:** Vaccination is recommended to prevent infection with high-risk HPV, a primary cause of penile cancer. <br>• **Specific Clinical Indication:** Primary prevention of HPV infection. <br>• **Treatment Modality:** HPV vaccination. <br>• **Additional Clinical Clarification:** Early vaccination of boys is recommended (e.g., at ages 11-13) to provide protection before the onset of sexual activity and potential exposure to HPV. |
| **Prevention / Risk Reduction** | **Safe Sexual Practices:** The use of male condoms is recommended as the most effective method for reducing the transmission of Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs) like HPV and HIV, which are established risk factors for penile cancer. |
