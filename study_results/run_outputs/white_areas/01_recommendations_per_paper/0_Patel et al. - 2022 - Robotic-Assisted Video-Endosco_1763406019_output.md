| **Lymph Node Management (Standard of Care)** | **Open Inguinal Lymph-Node Dissection (OILND)** is traditionally considered the gold standard for the surgical management of inguinal lymph nodes in patients with Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC) of the penis requiring a lymphadenectomy. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Patient Selection for Surgery)** | For patients with penile SCC and a low risk of metastasis (e.g., clinically non-palpable nodes, cN0), strategies can be employed to avoid the morbidity of a full lymphadenectomy: <br> • **Inguinal lymphadenectomy avoidance:** May be considered for patients identified as having the lowest risk of metastasis based on validated scoring systems. <br> • **Active surveillance or Dynamic Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy (dSLNB):** Can be used to predict and identify occult lymph node metastasis. <br> **Additional Clinical Clarification:** These less invasive strategies carry an inherent risk of missing micro-metastasis, which could lead to compromised oncological outcomes. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Choice of Surgical Technique)** | **Robotic-Assisted (RAVEIL) or standard Video-Endoscopic (VEIL) Inguinal Lymphadenectomy** is recommended as a viable alternative to Open Inguinal Lymph-Node Dissection (OILND) for reducing morbidity. <br> **Specific Clinical Indications:** This applies to patients with penile SCC who are candidates for inguinal lymphadenectomy. Some studies have used lymph node size >3 cm as an exclusion criterion for the minimally invasive approach. <br> **Treatment Modality with Precise Conditions:** The choice of RAVEIL/VEIL over OILND is justified by significantly lower rates of: <br> • Skin complications (wound infection, skin necrosis). <br> • Lymphedema. <br> • Major post-operative complications (Clavien-Dindo III-IV). <br> RAVEIL/VEIL also leads to shorter hospital stays and reduced duration of drainage, though these findings should be interpreted with caution due to study heterogeneity. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Oncological Equivalence)** | **RAVEIL/VEIL is considered oncologically equivalent to OILND** based on available evidence. <br> **Additional Clinical Clarification:** RAVEIL/VEIL provides an adequate lymph node yield and has demonstrated comparable outcomes in terms of recurrence rates, number of deaths, and overall survival when compared to the open technique. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Implementation and Practice)** | Due to its technical demands and associated learning curve, it is recommended that **minimally invasive inguinal lymphadenectomy (RAVEIL/VEIL) be performed in high-volume, centralized centers**. This ensures that surgical teams are proficient, leading to optimal patient safety and oncological outcomes. |
