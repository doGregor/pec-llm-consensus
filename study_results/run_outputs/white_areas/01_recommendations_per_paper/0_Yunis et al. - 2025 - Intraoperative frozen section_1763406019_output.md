Based on the systematic review provided, the following explicit treatment recommendations and descriptions of standard clinical practices for penile cancer have been extracted and organized.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Treatment** | **Penile-Preserving Surgery (PPS):** <br> • **Specific Clinical Indications:** For penile cancer (PeCa) where it is possible to completely excise the tumour and maintain a functional penile length.<br> • **Treatment Modality:** Penile-Preserving Surgery (PPS), which includes procedures like partial penectomy, radical circumcision, wide local excision (WLE), glansectomy, and glans resurfacing. |
| **Surgical Margins** | **Standard Excision Margins (EAU/ASCO Recommendation):** <br> • **Specific Clinical Indications:** For all excisional surgeries for penile cancer. <br> • **Treatment Modality with Precise Conditions:** Standard excision should encompass a margin of clinically normal-appearing skin around the tumour and any surrounding erythema to achieve complete removal with histologically negative margins. <br> • **Additional Clinical Clarifications:** Intraoperative Frozen Section Examination (FSE) can be used to allow for more conservative macroscopic margins and immediate re-resection if a margin is positive. |
| **Intraoperative Management** | **Use of Intraoperative Frozen Section Examination (FSE):** <br> • **Specific Clinical Indications:** During penile cancer surgery (including PPS, radical penectomy, and total penectomy) where resection margins are uncertain, to confirm complete excision. <br> • **Treatment Modality with Precise Conditions:** Intraoperative Frozen Section Examination (FSE) is performed to assess margin status. Its use during total penectomy is at the discretion of the surgeon or per departmental policy. <br> • **Additional Clinical Clarifications:** Close communication between the surgeon and histopathologist is critical for accurate assessment. |
| **Intraoperative Management** | **Specific Biopsy Protocols during Penile-Preserving Surgery (PPS) with FSE:** <br> • **Specific Clinical Indications:** During glansectomy with split-thickness skin graft (STSG). <br> • **Treatment Modality:** Biopsies of the complete circumference of the urethra and of each corporal tip should be sent for FSE. <br> • **Specific Clinical Indications:** During total glans resurfacing. <br> • **Treatment Modality:** Spongiosal biopsies of the deep margin should be taken for FSE. |
| **Follow-up Care** | **Surveillance Schedule after Penile-Preserving Surgery (PPS):** <br> • **Specific Clinical Indications:** Applicable after curative Penile-Preserving Surgery (PPS). <br> • **Additional Clinical Clarifications:** Follow-up is for monitoring local recurrence. <br> • **Treatment Modality with Precise Conditions:** Follow-up should be conducted every 3 months for the first 2 years, and then every 6 months until at least 5 years post-operatively. |
| **Patient Counseling** | **Informed Consent regarding FSE:** <br> • **Specific Clinical Indications:** For all patients undergoing surgery where Intraoperative Frozen Section Examination (FSE) is planned. <br> • **Treatment Modality with Precise Conditions:** Patients must be informed about the negative implications of FSE, specifically that discordance between FSE and final histology can occur, which may lead to early local recurrence. |
