Based on the systematic review and meta-analysis provided, the following treatment recommendations for surgical inguinal nodal staging in clinically node-negative (cN0) penile carcinoma can be extracted.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lymph Node Management (Preferred Surgical Staging)** | For patients with clinically node-negative (cN0) penile cancer requiring surgical staging (indicated for intermediate- or high-risk tumors, specifically stage ≥T1b or considered for grade 2 T1a), **Dynamic Sentinel Node Biopsy (DSNB)** is the favored procedure. It demonstrates good diagnostic accuracy and the lowest postoperative complication rates compared to other forms of inguinal lymph node dissection (ILND). This approach is especially accurate when performed in high-volume, experienced centers. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Adjunct to DSNB)** | When DSNB is planned for cN0 patients, **preoperative inguinal Ultrasound (US) and Fine-Needle Aspiration Cytology (FNAC)** of nodes with abnormal findings should be performed. This combination improves the diagnostic power of the staging process and can reduce the need for DSNB in cases where nodes are found to be positive, allowing for earlier therapeutic ILND. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Alternative to DSNB)** | If Dynamic Sentinel Node Biopsy (DSNB) is **not available or not preferred by the patient**, an inguinal lymph node dissection (modified ILND or ILND) can be considered as an alternative surgical staging option. |
| **Lymph Node Management (Surgical Technique for ILND)** | When an inguinal lymph node dissection (ILND) is indicated, a minimally invasive approach such as **Video-Endoscopic Inguinal Lymphadenectomy (VEIL) or Robot-Assisted VEIL (RA-VEIL)** should be considered over a traditional open ILND. VEIL/RA-VEIL is a feasible and safer alternative, associated with comparable diagnostic outcomes but significantly lower wound-related complication rates (including infection and skin necrosis) and fewer major complications. |
