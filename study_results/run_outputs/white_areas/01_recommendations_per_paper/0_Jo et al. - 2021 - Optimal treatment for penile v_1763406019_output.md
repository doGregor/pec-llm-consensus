Based on the provided paper, the following treatment recommendations are explicitly stated:

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Diagnosis** | For cases where Penile Verrucous Carcinoma (PVC) is highly suspected, repeated deeper biopsies that include the basement membrane of the papillomatous tumor are recommended to avoid misdiagnosis. |
| **Staging & Imaging** | For biopsy-confirmed PVC with no clinically palpable inguinal lymph nodes, further workups like CT or ultrasonography can be postponed initially. High-end MRI can be reserved for later use if needed to investigate tumor depth. |
| **Primary Treatment** | **Local excision with minimal surgical margins** followed by careful observation is recommended as the **first-line choice of treatment**. This is especially recommended for tumors: <br>• Measuring **< 3 cm** <br>• Classified as **Stage T1** (according to the 2016 TNM classification). |
| **Primary Treatment** | For tumors considered not to be PVC based on clinical or pathological findings, the **excision should be widened**. |
| **Primary Treatment** | For **Stage T2** penile cancers (which are defined as invasive cancers different from PVC), **aggressive treatment is recommended**. |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Prophylactic inguinal lymphadenectomy is not an appropriate treatment** for patients with non-palpable lymph nodes. |
| **Lymph Node Management** | For patients presenting with lymphadenopathy (palpable nodes), treatment with **anti-inflammatory drugs may be the treatment of choice, followed by a lymph node biopsy as needed**. |
| **Follow-up Care** | After a less aggressive treatment such as local excision, **close follow-up is essential** for the early detection of any sign of recurrence that may require additional resection. |
