Abstract

Background Inguinal lymph node dissection (ILND) plays a crucial role in the oncological management of patients
with melanoma, penile, and vulvar cancer. This study aims to systematically evaluate perioperative adverse events
(AEs) in patients undergoing ILND and its reporting.
Methods A systematic review was conducted according to PRISMA. PubMed, MEDLINE, Scopus, and Embase
were queried to identify studies discussing perioperative AEs in patients with melanoma, penile, and vulvar cancer
following ILND.
Results Our search generated 3.469 publications, with 296 studies meeting the inclusion criteria. Details of 14.421
patients were analyzed. Of these studies, 58 (19.5%) described intraoperative AEs (iAEs) as an outcome of interest.
Overall, 68 (2.9%) patients reported at least one iAE. Postoperative AEs were reported in 278 studies, combining data
on 10.898 patients. Overall, 5.748 (52.7%) patients documented C1 postoperative AEs. The most reported ILNDrelated AEs were lymphatic AEs, with a total of 4.055 (38.8%) events. The pooled meta-analysis confirmed that high
BMI (RR 1.09; p = 0.006), C1 comorbidities (RR 1.79; p = 0.01), and diabetes (RR 1.81; p = `\` 0.00001) are
independent predictors for any AEs after ILND. When assessing the quality of the AEs reporting, we found 25% of
studies reported at least 50% of the required criteria.
Conclusion ILND performed in melanoma, penile, and vulvar cancer patients is a morbid procedure. The quality of
the AEs reporting is suboptimal. A more standardized AEs reporting system is needed to produce comparable data
across studies for furthering the development of strategies to decrease AEs.

Introduction

Melanoma, penile, and vulvar cancers represent approximately
0.6–6% of all malignancies in the United States. Cumulatively,
a total of 108.180 new cases are expected in 2022 [1].
According to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Guidelines [2–4], inguinal lymph node
dissection (ILND) represents the standard treatment in
patients with intermediate (T1b) and high-risk (T2 and T3)
tumors, positive dynamic sentinel LN, or pathologically
confirmed ILN metastasis for malignancies of the penis,
vulva, and melanoma [2–4].
ILND is a diagnostic and therapeutic intervention,
playing a crucial role in the oncological management of
these patients. However, it is associated with high perioperative morbidity, with a wide reported incidence (3–97%)

[5]. There has been a sustained effort to decrease the
morbidity of this surgery, leading to the proposal of smaller
resection templates, preservation of the saphenous vein,
and minimally invasive approaches [6–8]. Nonetheless,
adverse events (AEs) are still a common occurrence [9].
Heterogeneity in AE assessment across the literature
might contribute to a lack of consistency and, therefore,
comparability between studies.
This study aims to systematically evaluate perioperative
AEs in patients with melanoma, penile, and vulvar cancer
undergoing ILND to answer three key questions: KQ1—
How is perioperative morbidity reported, assessed, and
graded; KQ2—What is the impact of different surgical
approaches, surgical techniques, and patient characteristics
on perioperative morbidity; KQ3—Are there predictors of
complications in patients undergoing ILND?

Evidence acquisition

Study design

Figure 1 depicts the workflow of the present study. We
performed a systematic review to identify all clinical
prospective randomized controlled trials (RCT) and nonrandomized trials and retrospective comparative and noncomparative studies reporting morbidity following ILND in
patients with melanoma, penile, and vulvar cancer. Subsequently, we critically evaluated the way complications were
reported in the studies identified from the literature review
using the Martin Criteria (10-item guidelines for reporting
surgical complications) [10] and the European Association
of Urology (EAU) ad hoc panel for complication reporting

[11]. To answer the PICOTS (Population, Intervention,
Comparators, Outcomes, Timing, and Setting) of the present
study, we first performed a pooled analysis to explore the


type of AEs primarily related to ILND. After an internal
discussion among the steering committee of the present
study, we defined intraoperative and postoperative AEs as
‘‘strongly,’’ ‘‘likely,’’ and ‘‘unlikely’’ related to the ILND.
Also, the AEs were categorized into five main clusters:
Lymphatic, Cutaneous, Infectious, Functional, and Vascular
(see below for definitions). Next, to assess the impact of
surgical approach and patient characteristics on ILND-related perioperative AEs, we performed an intervention metaanalysis of studies comparing open-ILND (O-ILND) vs.
minimally invasive ILND (MIS-ILND) and an inverse
variance meta-analysis of predictors for perioperative morbidity related to patient characteristics, respectively (see
below for definition).

ILND-related adverse events definition

Adverse events are defined as, ‘‘any unfavorable outcome
that occurs during or after the use of a drug or other

intervention and the causal relation between the interven
tion, and the event is at least a reasonable possibility’’

[12]. As part of these events, complications are stated as
‘‘[..] adverse events or effects following surgical and other
invasive intervention’’ [12].
As previously described [13], we provide a standardized
definition to better classify the morbidity of patients
undergoing ILND:

(a) Strongly ILND-related AEs: any perioperative complication directly associated with the violation of the
lymphatic system, not explainable otherwise (i.e.,
symptomatic lymphoceles or vascular injury to the
iliac vessels).
(b) Likely ILND-related AEs: any perioperative complication that is likely, but not exclusively, associated
with the violation of the lymphatic system that has
been reported even in the absence of ILND (i.e.,
venous thrombosis).
(c) Unlikely ILND-related AEs: any perioperative complication that cannot be directly or indirectly associated with the violation of the lymphatic system (i.e.,
hematuria).

These definitions are further categorized as Lymphatic,
Cutaneous, Infectious, Functional, and Vascular.

Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [14], and PRISMA Harms statements [12].
The present study is registered in PROSPERO
(CRD42021252602).

Results

Evidence synthesis

The electronic search identified 3.469 records. From these,
1.236 studies were selected for detailed review. This yielded 296 publications reporting perioperative AEs associated with the performance of ILND that met the inclusion
criteria for the pooled analysis. A total of 192 (64.8%)
retrospective studies, 36 (12.1%) case reports, 48 (16.2%)
prospective non-RCT studies, and 12 (4.1%) RCTs were
included. A total of 51 (17.2%) publications were comparative studies, of which 16 (5.4%) compared O-ILND
with MIS-ILND (Fig. 2).
This systematic review and pooled analysis included a
total of 14.421 patients from 348 cohorts of patients
(Supplementary materials—Tables 3 and 4). Most patients
had melanoma (46.8%), followed by vulvar (30.6%) and
penile carcinomas (21.9%). Considering the 283 records
(11.161 patients) that reported the percentage of patients
experiencing perioperative AEs, 5.873 patients (52.6%)
experienced C1 AEs and there were a total of 7060 AEs
(mean of 1.2 AEs/patient).

Discussion

This systematic review confirmed that ILND is a highly
morbid procedure impacted by both surgical and patient
factors. Across studies, there are heterogeneity and a lack
of consistency in the quality of the assessment, grading and
reporting of perioperative AEs.
Previous publications have shown that the quality of
reporting perioperative AEs is inadequate [10, 11, 21–23].
The reporting of AEs after ILND may vary because there is

no standard method. This has led to different nomencla
tures for the same complications, limiting the ability to
compare studies and perform accurate analyses, further
limiting the development of preventative and/or management strategies. Choosing a proper outcome reporting tool
is of the utmost importance [13].
Within the past decade, several tools for assessing,
reporting, and grading perioperative AEs have been proposed [11, 24–27]. Previous studies showed that despite
recent developments in AE reporting systems, they are not
used widely in clinical practice [28]. A potential reason
could be that not all journals have a standardized requirement for perioperative AEs reporting. An ongoing bibliometric analysis (data not published) showed that `\` 50% of
journal author guidelines (out of 1.400 journals analyzed)
require proper perioperative AE reporting [13, 15, 18, 28].
## 123


Our study indicated a sub-optimal usage of AE reporting
tools, with only 58 studies considering iAEs as an outcome

of interest.

In this study, there was a slight improvement in the
reporting of perioperative AEs over time. Martin et al. in
2002 [10] introduced 10 criteria, and subsequently Mitropoulos et al. in 2012 [11] provided 14 criteria with the goal
of improving perioperative AEs reporting. However, since
the latter was developed by urologists, it is possible that
those criteria were not widely applicable to the greater
surgical community. We performed a sub-analysis looking
only at the studies evaluating ILND for penile cancer
performed by urologists and showed similar results compared to the reporting of other urologic–oncologic surgeries [13, 15, 18, 28], highlighting that current
perioperative AEs classification systems are not enough

and more work in standardization is needed.

Our systematic review found that around 19.5% of
studies reported iAEs as an outcome of interest, and none
of these studies used published iAE reporting tools

[11, 24–27]. The low rate of iAEs reporting may be due to
a lack of consensus for how to report iAEs [21, 22, 28–32].
This assumption is partially based on previous publications
that found iAE grading tools are used 270 times less than
postoperative adverse events, which consistently use the
Clavien–Dindo classification system [31].

Among the postoperative AEs, the most common lymphatic AE was limb lymphedema [defined as ‘‘increased
limb circumference greater than 10% compared before the
surgery and at postoperative day 30’’ [33]], reported in
62.5% of the studies. The incidence of limb lymphedema
varies across studies [34], depending on the definition and
length of follow-up. For this reason, a standardized


definition should be used across all studies. Furthermore,
action should be taken to reduce the occurrence of lymphedema: lymph flow imaging techniques may identify
lymphatic leaks intraoperatively [33, 35, 36] and early
ambulation and the use of elastic stockings may minimize
lower extremity lymphedema [37, 38].

Seroma, defined as ‘‘a finding of tumor-like collection of
serum in the tissues’’ [39], is included in 41% of the studies
that reported cutaneous AEs as an outcome of interest. To
prevent this complication, most studies propose using
postoperative suction drain until the 24-h output becomes
less than 50 mL [40, 41].
Studies report wound infection-related AEs rates from
0.6 to 43% [9, 42]. Based on the results of our metaanalysis, we can infer that strict diabetes control is crucial
to avoiding this AEs. Inguinal paresthesia was noted in
60.6% of the studies that included functional AEs as an

outcome of interest. However, the incidence of these AEs
following ILND is rarely mentioned in the series [43, 44].
Vascular AEs were the least reported postoperative AEs
‘‘likely’’ related to ILND. Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)
(71.9%) was the most reported in this category. Patients
undergoing ILND may be at high risk of developing DVT
because of the nature of the surgery, underlying malignancy, and prolonged postoperative bed rest [44].
It is well known that ILND is associated with high morbidity [5, 45]. However, the rate of AEs may vary accordingly to the surgical approach, the technique used, and
patient characteristics. Patients undergoing MIS approaches
tend to have fewer wound complications, shorter hospital
stays, quicker returns to baseline functional status, and better
cosmesis, and MIS-ILND is no exception [46].
Comparisons between O-ILND and MIS-ILND are sparse
in the literature. AEs following O-ILND occur 80–100% of
the time and are predominantly cutaneous or lymphatic
related. Studies have shown that MIS-ILND approaches are
associated with a reduction in wound-related AEs, length of
stay, postoperative pain, and overall morbidity without
compromising oncological outcomes [8, 40, 47]. Our
cumulative analysis showed the occurrence of any postoperative AE was lower in patients who underwent MIS-ILND.
All categories of postoperative AEs trended toward more
favorable MIS outcomes, though the only statistically significant differences were in cutaneous and infectious-related
AEs. Despite a greater usage of MIS approaches for several
surgical procedures [13, 15, 18, 48], the overall use of MIS
for ILND remains limited [49]. This may be due to a steep
learning curve and its low incidence in countries where
robotic systems are not widely available.
Importantly, several studies have reported multivariable
analyses evaluating the predictors for any perioperative
AEs related to ILND (9, 50–54). The most common
reported predictors in the literature were age, BMI, diabetes, smoking status, C1 comorbidity, number of LNs
removed, sparing of the greater saphenous vein, and sartorius muscle transposition. When comparing the RRs of
these predictors, our inverse variance meta-analysis
showed that BMI, diabetes, and patients with C1 comorbidity reached statistical significance.
## 123


Limitations of this study include a significant heterogeneity between studies in terms of the lack of details on
the definition of different AEs or surgical approaches used.
There is not a standard way of performing an ILND; this

can introduce several confounders to our data. These

findings should be interpreted with caution given the high
heterogeneity in the methods/surgical techniques used, the
selection biases in the decision of performing one approach
versus another one (open vs. MIS), and the reporting of the
complications in the individual papers with various sample
sizes. Moreover, the patients included in our analysis had
three different types of cancers. Despite similarities in the
surgical approach, we do not know the impact of the differences in the biological aggressiveness of the cancers on
patient outcomes. Nevertheless, the results of this metaanalysis should be seen as a picture of the literature that
can serve as a roadmap for future comparisons and the
development of standardization tools.

Conclusion

ILND performed in patients with melanoma, penile, and
vulvar cancer is a very morbid procedure, with about 50%
of patients experiencing at least one AE. Lymphatic,
cutaneous, and infectious complications are the most
reported AEs. While there has been an improvement, the
quality of the AEs reporting is suboptimal. MIS-ILND is
associated with fewer skin and infectious AEs. Patients and

surgical factors have been found to be correlated with
perioperative AEs. A new standardized and comprehensive
classification system with common terminology is needed.
