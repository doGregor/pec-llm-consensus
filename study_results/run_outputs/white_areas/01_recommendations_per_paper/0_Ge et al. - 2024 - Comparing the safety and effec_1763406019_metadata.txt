------- | --------
**Systemic Therapy (Adjuvant)** | Adjuvant radiotherapy is recommended for improved oncological outcomes in pN3 squamous penile cancer.
**Systemic Therapy (Neoadjuvant)** | Neoadjuvant chemotherapy with triple therapy (including cisplatin and taxane) is recommended for enlarged or large inguinal lymph nodes in late-stage disease to reduce tumor burden before surgical evaluation.
**Lymph Node Management (Metastatic Disease)** | Radical lymphadenectomy and adjuvant chemotherapy are recommended for patients with confirmed lymphatic metastases.
**Lymph Node Management (Staging for cN0)** | Aggressive staging is recommended for non-palpable lymph nodes based on the risk factors of the primary tumor. In patients with clinically node-negative (cN0) disease, dynamic sentinel lymph node biopsy (DSNB) is indicated if available. Where DSNB is not available, surgical staging of the cN0 groin is still strongly recommended (modified open inguinal lymph node dissection is usually used). Current evidence supports minimally invasive ILND for cN0 disease.
**Lymph Node Management (Palpable Nodes / Lymphadenopathy)** | Open inguinal lymph node dissection (O-ILND) remains the standard approach for palpable inguinal nodes. Radical ILND is recommended for patients with inguinal lymphadenopathy.
**Lymph Node Management (Advanced Disease)** | In N2/N3 disease, lymph node metastases should be treated with radical surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy.
**Lymph Node Management (Recurrence)** | Neoadjuvant chemotherapy and radical lymph node surgery are recommended for recurrence of inguinal lymph nodes after radical ILND.
**Lymph Node Management (Surgical Modality for cN1-2)** | Opt for open surgery (O-ILND) instead of minimally invasive ILND in patients with cN1-2 disease to reduce the risk of large lymph node fractures. Minimally invasive ILND for this stage should be part of a clinical trial.
**Lymph Node Management (General Surgical Approach)** | In summary, minimally invasive surgery (MIS) is a better option than O-ILND for inguinal lymph node dissection for suitable penile cancer cases, with the advantages of a shorter length of stay, less blood loss, and fewer complications, with comparable oncological outcomes.
