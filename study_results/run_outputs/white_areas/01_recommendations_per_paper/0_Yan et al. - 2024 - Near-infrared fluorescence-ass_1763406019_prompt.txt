**Xiaoting Yan** **[1,2]** **[· Chao Liu](http://orcid.org/0000-0002-5976-131X)** **[3]** **· Lijuan Cui** **[3]** **· Pengyu Yan** **[1]** **· Xiurong Fu** **[1]** **· Weiyi Chen** **[4]** **· Xiaofeng Yang** **[1,2]**

**Abstract**
**Objective** Identification of superficial inguinal lymph nodes during low-risk penile cancer surgery using near-infrared (NIR)
fluorescence to improve the accuracy of lymph-node dissection and reduce the incidence of missed micrometastases and
complications.
**Methods**
Thirty-two cases were selected, which were under the criteria of < T1, and no lymph-node metastasis was found
with magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) detection. Two groups were randomly divided based on the fluorescence technique,
the indocyanine green (ICG) group and the non-ICG group. In the ICG group, the ICG preparation was subcutaneously
injected into the edge of the penile tumor 10 min before surgery, and the near-infrared fluorescence imager was used for
observation. After the lymph nodes were visualized, the superficial inguinal lymph nodes were removed first, and then, the
penis surgery was performed. The non-ICG group underwent superficial inguinal lymph-node dissection and penile surgery.
**Results**
Among the 16 patients in the ICG group, we obtained 11 lymph-node specimens using grayscale values of images
*P* *P*
(4.13 ± 0.72 vs. 3.00 ± 0.82 = 0.003) along with shorter postoperative healing time (7.31 ± 1.08 vs. 8.88 ± 2.43 = 0.025),
and less lymphatic leakage (0 vs. 5 *P* = 0.04) than the 16 patients in the non-ICG group. Out of 11, 3 lymph nodes that are
excised were further grouped into fluorescent and non-fluorescent regions (G1/G2) and found to be metastasized.
**Conclusion**
Near-infrared fluorescence-assisted superficial inguinal lymph-node dissection in penile carcinoma is accurate
and effective, and could reduce surgical complications.

The metastatic route of penile cancer is mainly lymph-node
metastasis and has the characteristics of cascading along
superficial inguinal lymph nodes–deep inguinal lymph
nodes–external iliac lymph nodes–pelvic lymph nodes.
Whether regional lymph nodes metastasize, the number of
positive lymph nodes and the timing of regional lymph-node
excision are the most important prognostic factors affecting
the survival of patients with penile cancer [1, 2]. Patients
with stage less than T1N0 penile cancer have a better overall prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate of 85–100% [3].
Most scholars believe that in high risk especially palpable
lymph nodes in stage ­pT 1b -pT 4 patients, sentinel lymph-node
biopsy or radical lymph-node excision is recommended. In
contrast, patients in low-risk inguinal lymph nodes (cN0)
without enlargement but with tumor stages of Tis, Ta, and
pT1a should be regularly monitored [4, 5]. However, retrospective analyses have shown that approximately 15–25%

of (cN0) patients may still have micrometastatic disease,
and as a result, the survival rate for patients with recurrence during follow-up is low at 35%, and the disease-free
survival rate for patients with lymph-node micrometastases is 84% if lymph-node excision is performed at the early
stage [6]. The clinical management of hard-to-find inguinal lymph nodes (cN0) is controversial [7]. Blind inguinal
lymph-node excision in cN0 patients at high risk of micrometastases can also lead to excessive treatment in 80% of
patients [8], as lymphatic fluid exuding from the wound can
delay wound healing and lead to associated complications.
This is a more problematic issue for the patient and the care
involved. Fluorescence-assisted precise identification of
lymph nodes is now well established, and patients with < T1
stage will benefit most from precise identification of inguinal lymph nodes and minimal harm in clearing this area.
Accurate lymph-node excision is an important technique in
precision medicine and is becoming the standard for biopsy,
excision, and staging of lymph nodes in many solid tumor
areas. Near-infrared (NIR) fluorescence imaging has evolved
into an efficient approach for examining tumor tissue during
surgery. NIR fluorescence (700–900 nm) imaging is being
used to localize anterior lymph nodes and has been tested in
several cancer centers worldwide [9]. The features of NIR
fluorescence imaging that can facilitate lymph-node surgery
include relatively high penetration into living tissue (up to
10 mm) and real-time, high-resolution optical guidance [10].
Also, the diagnostic performance of ICG-NIR imaging for
SLNM detection in penile cancer was analyzed in seven
studies earlier where the median sensitivity and specificity
of ICG-NIR imaging for SLNM detection were 100 and 4%,
respectively; the pooled sensitivity was 100.0% (95% confidence interval [CI] 97.0–100.0) and specificity was 2.0%
(95% CI 1.0–3.0). There was no significant difference in
the diagnostic results between different injection sites and
doses in each experimental group. ICG is sensitive in the
imaging of SLN tissue, which can consequently improve
the accuracy of lymph-node detection [11]. Therefore, we
began investigating the implementation of NIR fluorescenceassisted superficial inguinal lymph-node excision in patients
with primary tumor Ta, T1 (cN0) to detect micrometastases
earlier while reducing complications associated with lymph
node excision.

**Materials and methods**

**Patient selection**

Approved by the ethics committee of the First Hospital
of Shanxi Medical University. Thirty-two patients aged
between 38 and 71 years old with penile cancer attending
the Urology Department of First Hospital of Shanxi Medical


University (Shanxi, China) from January 2018 to December

2021 were selected. The inclusion criteria were as follows:

cancer to be located in the glans or coronal sulcus and foreskin, cancer diameter of 1.8–3 cm, and the mean diameter to
be 2.21 cm, no palpable inguinal lymph nodes on physical
examination, no suspicious metastatic lymph nodes on MRI,
no abnormal blood glucose (based on glycated hemoglobin
test after admission and seven blood glucose monitoring
within 3 days), no cardiovascular disease (no anticoagulant
drugs were intaken), and clinical tumor stage to be ≤ stage
T1.
After confirming that the patients met the inclusion criteria, we randomly divided the patients into ICG and non-ICG
groups with a ratio of 1:1. Sixteen patients in the ICG group
were injected with the prepared ICG solution intradermally
10 min before surgery and underwent superficial inguinal
lymph-node excision followed by partial penile excision
after lymph-node visualization. In the non-ICG group, 16
patients underwent direct partial penectomy and superficial
inguinal lymph-node excision. In both groups, the superficial
inguinal lymph-node excision was carried out by an inguinal
lymphadenectomy surgery otherwise called groin dissection.
The procedure was performed by the same team of trained
and experienced urologists.

**Surgical procedure**

0.25 ml (0.25 mg/ml) of ICG solution was injected intradermally into the patient’s penile head at the site of cancer using
a 1 ml syringe. The development and tracking of lymph
nodes were performed by the self-developed Multispectral
Fluorescence imaging System from our team (MFS). The
system consists of a light source, an image processor and
a displayer. The light source can output visible and nearinfrared light at 760 ± 10 nm simultaneously. Visible color
imaging and NIR fluorescence imaging (835 ± 10 nm) can
be achieved, and then, the color images, NIR fluorescence
images, and pseudo-color images (green) could be acquired
simultaneously and displayed on the monitor.
ICG preparation was injected intradermally at the tumor
margin. The MFS probe is aimed at the injection site and
the dynamics of the fluorescent images are observed. The
monitor shows a visible image, an indocyanine greenlabeled near-infrared fluorescent grayscale image and a
green pseudo-color image at the injection site, the penile
body, the pubic symphysis, and the bilateral inguinal
region simultaneously. The cancer foci development is first
observed (Fig. 1A), and after 15 min, the green-fluorescent
image of the superficial lymph nodes in the inguinal region
was stable (Fig. 1C, D). After cutting the skin, the color of
the green nodes is clearly enhanced (Fig. 1B). Thus, the
surgeon is able to easily find more lymph nodes based on
the fluorescence image and can accurately separate and

**Statistical analysis**

The study has been powered using a two independent proportions power analysis. The used outcome was the proportion of fluorescent technique, the indocyanine green
(ICG) group compared to the non-ICG group (ICG). For
statistical analysis, IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 25, La
Jolla, CA, USA) was used. For the secondary outcomes, an
intention-to-treat analysis was performed. To evaluate the
differences between the standard and fluorescence imaging
groups, *t* test (two-tailed parametric) was used. *P* < 0.05
was considered as significant.

**Quantitative mode of fluorescence images**

The final image on the screen during the operation is formed
by the fusion of visible light image and fluorescent image,
and the images of each mode can also be displayed separately. The fluorescence generated by indocyanine green is
defined as the continuous bright area under the NIR gray
image that does not change with the observation angle. The
fluorescence imager can digitize the fluorescence information of the image to generate the average gray value. In the
later stage, the mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of adjacent tissues can be calculated by the average gray value.
The ratio of tumor tissue MFI to normal background tissue
MFI was the tumor-to-background ratio (TBR). We selected
11 lymph-node specimens isolated from the ICG group and
obtained the grayscale values of the fluorescent region G1
and the non-fluorescent region G2 on the same lymph node
under the fluorescence imager (the grayscale values and the
average fluorescence intensity were linearly and positively
correlated). Set the maximum grayscale value as 255 and
the minimum grayscale value as 0, and normalize the gray
value to obtain the range of 0–1 (Fig. 4). The red column is
G1, and the blue is G2. The grayscale value of the visible
fluorescent region is significantly stronger than that of the
(G1/G2 > 5) non-fluorescent region. The 11 lymph nodes
were further divided into fluorescent and non-fluorescent
regions, and three of them were found to have metastasis
by pathological examination, and the grayscale values of
the cancer foci on the same lymph nodes were significantly
higher than the normal tissue adjacent to the cancer, which
was statistically analyzed using independent *t* test (twotailed parametric test). This provided an optical quantitative
basis for surgeons to identify lymph nodes through the use
of NIR fluorescence.

**Discussion**

In patients with primary penile cancer, about 50% of the
palpable lymph-node enlargement is caused by inflammatory reaction [12, 13]. For patients with T1cNo or below,
although the guidelines do not recommend the removal of
lymph nodes, almost 100% of the cases with lymph-node
enlargement during follow-up are caused by metastasis [14].
It shows that lymph-node resection of early penile cancer
is also necessary, but the complications make doctors and
patients think that the benefits outweigh the losses. More

advanced surgical methods would be the best treatment
option if the most complete removal of the lymph nodes
with the least amount of damage is provided. Lymph-node
excision in early stage (Ta-T1a) penile cancer has been a
matter of academic debate for many years. Patients opting
for active surveillance after resection of the primary lesion
may avoid the complications associated with prophylactic
inguinal lymph-node excision, while the treatment may be
delayed. If there is no lymph nodes metastasis after removal,
the delay in discharge and excessive treatment costs caused
by postoperative complications may attribute damage to the
patient’s body and spirit and also put the doctor in an awkward position. There are also several improved methods of
inguinal lymph-node dissection to reduce the probability of
postoperative lymphatic leakage, lymphoedema, flap necrosis, and wound infection [15]. These include laparoscopic
surgery, robot-assisted laparoscopic surgery, and the creation
of a vascularised flap. One set of data showed that the cosmetic outcome of the incision was better in the single-port
laparoscopy than in the conventional laparoscopic group
( *P* = 0.039) [16]. In a study comparing robotic-assisted endoscopic lymph-node dissection (RAVEIL) with conventional
endoscopic lymph-node dissection (VEIL), the former had
a significantly lower complication rate than the latter (7%
vs 43%), while RAVEIL allowed a greater probability of
preserving the saphenous vein compared to VEIL ( *P* < 0.01),
and the preservation of the saphenous vein reduced the complication rate ( *P* = 0.02) [17]. However, none of these methods have improved the surgical approach in the direction
of precise identification of lymph nodes. Numerous studies
have demonstrated the potential for NIR fluorescence imaging employing intraoperative ICG-99mTc-nanocolloid to
be incorporated into conventional lymph-node operations
in patients with low-risk cancer to ease the direct observation of those nodes. Why is ICG used in NIRF cancer
detection is an open question. In contrast to blue dyes,
which have poor screening, a few pilot studies have shown


that using hybrid tracer dyes, such as indocyanine green, in
NIRF imaging during surgical procedures accelerated the
process of tumor identification and its specific location for
a surgeon to aid in its removal. The level of perfusion, permeability, and absorption of ICG over methylene blue is
the fundamental basis for this advantage in tumor detection.
Non-malignant tissues quickly flush out the dye, which aids
in more accurate tumor development delineation [18]. The
application of NIR fluorescence provides a brighter direction for this idea, which is an improvement at the cellular
level. A study retrospectively analyzed the number of lymph
nodes obtained by robot-assisted surgery in ICG and nonICG groups and found that the number of overall, superficial,
and deep lymph nodes in the bilateral groin was higher in
the ICG group than in the non-ICG group ( *P* < 0.02) [19].
NIR fluorescence can penetrate the skin and reveal the location of subcutaneous lymph nodes and lymphatic vessels in
real time. The increased penetration of light after cutting
the skin makes the lymph-node-like tissue with a stronger
fluorescent signal visible, allowing the operator to separate
and remove lymph nodes along the fluorescent edge quickly
and accurately. A higher probability of lymph-node involvement was strongly correlated with a penile tumor size of
1 cm or more. In a study, by Marion et al., the use of NIRF in
vulvar cancer cases with capsular invasion was in the range
of ≤ 2 mm to > 2 mm. Given this, the NIRF was employed
in this research to visualize the predicted dimension of the

micrometastasized lymph node, i.e., of ≤ 2 mm [
spatial resolution of our imaging protocol can achieve detection in the order of 100 µm, so there is a high probability of
detection for lymph nodes smaller than 1 mm. In addition,
it can avoid unnecessary damage to other tissues, reduce
the incision, and shorten the operation time. This can better
avoid missing patients with no abnormalities on preoperative imaging but was actually having micrometastases, thus
to avoid unnecessary lymph-node dissection, and especially
to reduce the occurrence of postoperative complications,

lymph nodes was not statistically significant between ICG
and non-ICG ( *P*

It has been widely used for intraoperative lymph-node
exploration in other tumors, and near-infrared fluorescent
dyes combined with targeting antigens have been synthesized for the application of contrast agents in animal experiments of breast and prostate cancer [21, 22]. As the current
clinically available fluorescent contrast agent has a tissue
penetration of about 1 cm, it is not very effective for extracutaneous visualization in patients. In addition, the fluorescent
contrast agent may not show all lymph nodes in the target
area, so it can only be used in patients with a BMI within a
certain range. The present lymph-node visualization study
focuses on the inguinal lymph nodes, and the injection site is
at the edge of the tumor. This is a theoretical sentinel lymph
node; however, due to the limitations of our institution, it is
not possible to perform a preoperative [99m] Tc examination to
verify this result. This will require a multicentre and largesample study.

**Conclusion**

Our research supports that NIR fluorescence-assisted intraoperative lymph-node resection could accurately locate
lymph nodes, effectively remove more lymph nodes, reduce
surgical complications, and relieve patient’s physical and
mental pain. MFS could significantly improve the visual
depth of doctors and dynamically and accurately locate
lymph nodes in vitro and in vivo.
