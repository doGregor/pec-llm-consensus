Based on the systematic review and meta-analysis provided, the following treatment recommendations for penile cancer are identified. The paper's focus is exclusively on the surgical management of inguinal lymph nodes.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lymph Node Management / Staging** | **Specific Clinical Indication:** For patients with clinically LN-negative groins (cN0), where no suspicious palpable lymph nodes are detected.<br><br>**Treatment Modality:** Early lymph node surgery is recommended as it significantly improves outcomes and survival compared to a delayed surgery approach. |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Specific Clinical Indication:** For managing lymph node metastasis in patients with penile cancer.<br><br>**Treatment Modality:** Open Inguinal Lymphadenectomy (OIL) is considered the established 'gold standard' procedure and the standard of care against which other techniques are compared. |
| **Lymph Node Management** | **Specific Clinical Indication:** For patients with penile cancer requiring inguinal lymphadenectomy.<br><br>**Treatment Modality:** The findings of the meta-analysis support the use of Video Endoscopic Inguinal Lymphadenectomy (VEIL) over Open Inguinal Lymphadenectomy (OIL).<br><br>**Additional Clinical Clarifications:** VEIL is recommended due to significant advantages in perioperative outcomes, including fewer wound infections, less skin necrosis, a lower incidence of lymphoedema, shorter drainage time, and shorter hospital stays. The authors note that while these findings support the use of VEIL, the limited sample size across the reviewed studies calls for cautious interpretation and further research. |
