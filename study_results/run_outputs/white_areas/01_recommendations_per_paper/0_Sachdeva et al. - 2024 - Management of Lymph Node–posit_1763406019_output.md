Based on the systematic review provided, the following explicit treatment recommendations for lymph node-positive penile cancer have been identified and extracted.

| Aspect of Care | Recommendation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **General Management Principle** | Individualised management for patients with nodal disease should be discussed in an experienced multidisciplinary team setting. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Surgery)** | **Standard of Care:** Surgery is the mainstay of treatment. Radical inguinal lymph node dissection (ILND) is the standard of care for positive, resectable inguinal lymph nodes. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Surgery)** | **Timing of Surgery:** Early lymphadenectomy improves survival. Delaying ILND beyond 3 months is detrimental and should be avoided, particularly in patients with high-risk primary disease (Grade 3 or ≥T1b stage). |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Surgery)** | **Surveillance:** Surveillance or delayed ILND for patients with clinically suspected positive nodes (cN+) is not recommended as it risks missing a curative opportunity and is associated with higher regional recurrence rates compared to immediate ILND or RT. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Surgery)** | **Minimally Invasive Surgery:** Minimally invasive ILND (e.g., VEIL, RAVEIL) can be considered. It appears feasible and may reduce wound-related morbidity, but more data is needed to confirm long-term oncological safety in patients with clinically positive (cN+) disease. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Systemic Therapy)** | **Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy (NACT):** NACT is reserved for patients with fixed or bulky inguinal lymph nodes (cN2–3), particularly when the disease is not immediately resectable, with the goal of enabling subsequent surgical consolidation. It can also be considered in other resectable cN2-3 cases. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Systemic Therapy)** | **(Neo)adjuvant Therapy for Early Nodal Disease:** (Neo)adjuvant treatment (chemotherapy or radiotherapy) is not recommended for pN1 disease as it has no proven survival benefit. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Systemic Therapy)** | **Adjuvant Radiotherapy (ART):** ART should be considered for pN2–3 disease after ILND, as it may reduce recurrence and offer a survival benefit. A benefit is apparent in pN2 disease but not pN1 disease. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Systemic Therapy)** | **Adjuvant Chemotherapy (ACT):** ACT may be of benefit in pN2–3 disease, particularly for patients with pN3 disease, but is associated with considerable toxicity. It does not improve survival in pN1-2 disease. |
| **Inguinal Lymph Node Management (Systemic Therapy)** | **Adjuvant Chemoradiotherapy (ACRT):** ACRT may provide a modest additional benefit in reducing locoregional recurrence and improving survival in pN3 disease, especially in cases with extranodal extension (ENE) or HPV positivity. |
| **Pelvic Lymph Node Management** | **Indications for Pelvic Lymph Node Dissection (PLND):** PLND should be performed after ILND in patients with predictors for pelvic lymph node involvement, which include the presence of three or more positive inguinal nodes or extranodal extension (ENE). PLND improves outcomes in pN2 disease. |
| **Pelvic Lymph Node Management** | **Adjuvant Therapy after PLND:** Both adjuvant chemotherapy (ACT) and adjuvant radiotherapy (ART) show modest improvements in recurrence and survival outcomes after PLND. |
| **Pelvic Lymph Node Management** | **Adjuvant Chemotherapy (ACT) after PLND:** ACT is associated with a significant overall survival benefit in patients with pelvic lymph node metastases (pelvic pN3 disease) and should be considered. |
| **Pelvic Lymph Node Management** | **Adjuvant Radiotherapy (ART) after PLND:** ART may offer improved recurrence-free and overall survival in pN3 disease after PLND, particularly in the absence of extranodal extension (ENE). |
